EV vs Petrol Car Road Tax Comparison 2026

By DesiCalc Editorial Team · Last updated 30 October 2026

One of the biggest advantages of buying an electric vehicle (EV) in India is the significant savings on road tax. Most states offer full or partial road tax exemption for EVs as part of their EV policies. This guide compares EV vs petrol car road tax across all Indian states so you can calculate exactly how much you save by going electric.

Top States for EV Buyers (Full Exemption)

These states offer 100% road tax exemption on electric vehicles (subject to any price cap shown), making them the most affordable places to own an EV:

🚗 Delhi – Full EV Exemption

Delhi exempts road tax on EVs priced up to ₹30 lakh and also waives registration fees. Petrol cars pay 4–10% by price band; at ₹15 lakh the rate is 10%, so an EV saves about ₹1,50,000 in road tax alone.

🚗 Maharashtra – Full EV Exemption

Maharashtra waives 100% road tax on EVs. Petrol cars pay 11–13% by price band plus a 2% road safety cess; at ₹15 lakh the all-in road tax is about ₹1,83,600, which an EV buyer avoids entirely.

🚗 Tamil Nadu – Full EV Exemption

Tamil Nadu offers full road tax exemption for EVs. Petrol cars pay 12–20% by price band; at ₹15 lakh the rate is 18%, so an EV saves about ₹2,70,000 in road tax.

🚗 Uttar Pradesh – Full EV Exemption

UP offers 100% road tax exemption for EVs. Petrol cars pay 9–11%; at ₹15 lakh the rate is 11%, so an EV saves about ₹1,65,000 in road tax.

🚗 Rajasthan – Full EV Exemption

Rajasthan exempts EVs from road tax. Petrol cars pay 6–10% by engine capacity plus a 12.5% surcharge; at ₹15 lakh with a 1,200cc engine the road tax is about ₹1,51,875, which an EV buyer avoids.

🚗 Telangana – Full EV Exemption

Telangana offers full road tax exemption for EVs. Petrol cars pay 13–21% by price band plus a ₹5,000 road safety cess; at ₹15 lakh the road tax is about ₹2,75,000, so an EV saves about ₹2,70,000 after the cess.

States with Partial or Slabbed EV Concessions

These states offer concessions but not a blanket full exemption. The saving depends on the EV's price and, for Karnataka and Kerala, the price slab:

Gujarat – 1%

Gujarat charges 1% road tax for EVs versus 6% for petrol cars. On a ₹15L car, the EV tax is about ₹11,719 versus ₹70,313 for petrol — a saving of about ₹58,594.

Haryana – 100% up to ₹30L, 50% above

Haryana offers full road tax exemption for EVs priced up to ₹30 lakh and a 50% concession above that cap. On a ₹15L car, the EV road tax is ₹0 versus about ₹1,20,000 for petrol.

Karnataka – 5/8/10% slabs

Karnataka charges EV road tax on price slabs: 5% up to ₹10L, 8% from ₹10L–₹25L, and 10% above ₹25L. On a ₹15L car, the EV rate is 8% (about ₹1,34,200) versus about ₹2,84,050 for petrol including cess — a saving of about ₹1,49,850.

Kerala – 3/5/10/15% slabs

Kerala charges EV road tax on price slabs: 3% up to ₹10L, 5% from ₹10L–₹20L, 10% from ₹20L–₹40L, and 15% above ₹40L. On a ₹15L car, the EV rate is 5% (about ₹75,000) versus about ₹2,25,000 for petrol — a saving of about ₹1,50,000.

Madhya Pradesh – 4%

Madhya Pradesh charges 4% road tax for EVs versus 8–14% for petrol cars by price band. On a ₹15L car, the EV tax is about ₹60,000 versus ₹1,50,000 for petrol — a saving of about ₹90,000.

Chhattisgarh & Jharkhand – 25% concession

Both states give a 25% concession on road tax for EVs, not a full exemption. On a ₹15L car, the EV tax is about ₹67,500 (4.5%) versus ₹90,000 for petrol — a saving of about ₹22,500.

States with Limited or No EV Benefit

Most states in DesiCalc's 2026 rate data offer some EV benefit, but Arunachal Pradesh and Mizoram currently charge the same road tax for EVs as for petrol vehicles in the calculator. The smallest concession is the 25% road tax discount in Chhattisgarh and Jharkhand. Madhya Pradesh's 4% EV rate is a partial concession, while Gujarat's 1% rate is close to a full exemption. If you are comparing a high-priced EV, check the state's price slabs because the concession may be worth less than the headline suggests.

Chhattisgarh and Jharkhand – 25% concession

These two states reduce the petrol road tax rate by 25% for EVs rather than exempting them. At ₹15 lakh, the saving is about ₹22,500. Madhya Pradesh's 4% EV rate saves about ₹90,000, and Gujarat's 1% rate saves about ₹58,594.

Beyond Road Tax: Other EV Benefits You Should Know

Road tax is just one part of the EV cost advantage. Here are additional savings and benefits of going electric:

🆓 Registration Fee Waiver

Some states waive the vehicle registration fee for EVs in addition to road tax. In DesiCalc's 2026 data, Delhi, Punjab, Bihar, West Bengal, Odisha and Andhra Pradesh waive the EV registration fee; Maharashtra, Uttar Pradesh and Kerala do not. The fee is typically ₹600–₹1,100, so the waiver is a small add-on to the road tax saving.

💰 FAME II / EMPS Subsidy

The Government of India's FAME II scheme (and its successor EMPS — Electric Mobility Promotion Scheme 2024) provides upfront purchase subsidies for EVs. For cars, the subsidy is approximately ₹10,000-₹15,000 per kWh of battery capacity, subject to a maximum. For a 30kWh battery EV like the Tata Nexon EV, the subsidy can be ₹1.5L+ off the ex-showroom price.

🧾 Income Tax Deduction (Sec 80EEB)

Under Section 80EEB, you can claim an income tax deduction of up to ₹1.5L on the interest paid on an EV loan. This is available only in the old tax regime. If you take a ₹10L loan at 9% for 5 years, the first-year interest is around ₹80,000, which is fully deductible. This deduction is available for EV loans sanctioned before March 31, 2027.

🔌 Green Number Plate Benefits

EVs with green number plates get additional perks in many states: free parking in select malls and government lots (Delhi), toll fee concessions on some national highways, exemption from odd-even and other road rationing schemes, lower or waived green tax, and eligibility for HOV (high occupancy vehicle) lanes in some cities. These benefits add to the overall value proposition.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. Is EV road tax exemption available in all states?

No. Benefits range from full exemption (Delhi, Maharashtra, Tamil Nadu, Uttar Pradesh, Rajasthan, Telangana and others) to slabbed rates (Karnataka 5/8/10%, Kerala 3/5/10/15%), partial rates (Gujarat 1%, Madhya Pradesh 4%), a 25% concession (Chhattisgarh and Jharkhand), and Haryana's 100% up to ₹30 lakh then 50%. The situation is dynamic, so always check the latest rates for your state.

2. How much can I save on road tax by buying an EV?

For a ₹15L car, road tax savings range from about ₹22,500 in Chhattisgarh and Jharkhand to about ₹2,70,000 in Tamil Nadu and Telangana. Most full-exemption states save ₹90,000–₹2,70,000 on road tax alone, before any registration fee waiver or purchase incentive.

3. Is road tax calculated differently for EVs vs petrol cars?

Yes. For petrol cars, road tax is usually based on ex-showroom price and engine capacity (CC). For EVs, road tax is typically a flat percentage or a reduced percentage of the ex-showroom price (or fully exempt). There is no CC-based calculation for EVs since they don't have engine displacement.

4. Does road tax exemption apply to used EVs too?

In most states, road tax exemption applies only to the first registration of a new EV. When you transfer ownership of a used EV, the new owner typically has to pay a nominal transfer fee but not the full road tax. However, some states may charge a pro-rated road tax on transfer, so check your state's rules.

5. Will EV road tax exemption continue in the future?

Most state EV policies have a defined time horizon (e.g., 2025-2027). As EV adoption increases, states may phase out or reduce exemptions gradually. For example, Maharashtra reduced its EV concession from 100% to partial and then back to 100%. It's advisable to factor this uncertainty into your long-term ownership calculations, but for immediate purchases, the benefits are secure under current policies.
