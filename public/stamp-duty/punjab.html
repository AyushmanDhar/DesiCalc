Overview of Real Estate in Punjab

Punjab's real estate market is anchored by its major cities: Ludhiana, the industrial hub known for hosiery and bicycle manufacturing; Amritsar, home of the Golden Temple and a strong commercial centre; Jalandhar, a major sports goods and logistics hub; and Patiala, the cultural capital. The fastest-growing market is SAS Nagar (Mohali) and the Chandigarh periphery — Zirakpur, Kharar, Dera Bassi and New Chandigarh — which attract buyers priced out of Chandigarh proper while offering superior connectivity to the tricity region.

Development in Punjab is largely driven by GMADA (Greater Mohali Area Development Authority) and PUDA (Punjab Urban Development Authority), with planned townships, IT parks and the Aerocity near Chandigarh International Airport. Punjab has an active RERA framework that has brought more transparency to new launches, especially in Mohali and Ludhiana. Circle rates (collector rates) are notified by the state government and are revised periodically; they form the minimum valuation for stamp duty.

Punjab's stamp duty at 7% for male buyers is among the highest in north India, but the 2% concession for female buyers makes registration considerably cheaper for women. With registration at 1% (no cap), the total cost of registering a property in Punjab ranges from 6% for a female or all-female-joint buyer to 8% for a male or all-male-joint buyer.

How Stamp Duty is Calculated in Punjab

Stamp duty in Punjab is calculated on the higher of the agreement value or the circle (collector) rate notified for the locality. The standard rates are 7% for male buyers and 5% for female buyers. Joint ownership rates depend on composition: two female owners pay 5%, two male owners pay 7%, and mixed-gender joint ownership attracts 6%. Registration charges are a flat 1% of the property value with no upper cap.

For example, a male buyer of a ₹50 lakh property pays 7% stamp duty (₹3,50,000) plus 1% registration (₹50,000), for a total of ₹4,00,000. A female buyer of the same property pays 5% stamp duty (₹2,50,000) plus 1% registration (₹50,000), for a total of ₹3,00,000 — saving ₹1,00,000. A mixed-gender couple pays 6% stamp duty (₹3,00,000) plus registration (₹50,000), for a total of ₹3,50,000.

Property Registration Process in Punjab

The registration process in Punjab is managed through the state's Sub-Registrar (Tehsildar) offices. Here are the steps:

  • Step 1: Prepare the sale deed and get it drafted by a registered deed writer or advocate.
  • Step 2: Pay stamp duty through e-stamping via SHCIL (shcilestamp.com).
  • Step 3: Book an appointment at the Sub-Registrar office for the concerned tehsil, either online through the state's registration services or in person at the office.
  • Step 4: Appear at the Sub-Registrar office with all original documents. Both parties must be present with two witnesses. Biometric verification and photography are conducted.
  • Step 5: The deed is registered after verification and payment of the 1% registration fee. The registered document is returned along with the registration receipt.

Documents Required

  • Sale deed on e-stamp certificate
  • Previous title documents
  • Encumbrance certificate
  • Property tax receipts and house tax clearance
  • Aadhaar card and PAN card
  • Passport-size photographs
  • Two witnesses with identity proof
  • NOC from the society or development authority (GMADA/PUDA, if applicable)
