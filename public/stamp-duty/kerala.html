Overview of Real Estate in Kerala

Kerala's real estate market is shaped by high land values, limited availability of land, and strong demand from NRIs, particularly from the Gulf region. The state's high population density and the Western Ghats and Arabian Sea boundaries constrain land supply, pushing prices steadily upward in urban centres. Kochi, the commercial capital, leads the market with premium developments around Marine Drive, Kakkanad, and the emerging IT corridor. Thiruvananthapuram, the state capital, is driven by the Technopark IT hub and government employment, while Kozhikode, Thrissur, Kollam, and Kannur have active residential markets with strong local demand.

Kochi has emerged as the state's real estate powerhouse, with waterfront properties, gated communities, and commercial projects attracting both domestic and NRI investment. The Kochi Metro and the development of new business districts have boosted property values in nearby areas. Thiruvananthapuram's real estate benefits from the presence of Technopark and the Vizhinjam International Seaport, which are expected to drive further growth. In the Malabar region, Kozhikode and Kannur have seen steady appreciation driven by remittance income and local economic activity.

Kerala's unique fair value system means that government-fixed land values tend to track market prices closely in urban areas, reducing the gap between agreement values and taxable values. For buyers, this means the stamp duty outgo is largely predictable. The state also has a well-organised registration department, with all Sub-Registrar offices connected to the online IGR system.

How Stamp Duty is Calculated in Kerala

Stamp duty in Kerala is calculated on the higher of the transaction value or the fair value of land fixed by the state government. The fair value is published annually by the Inspector General of Registration and varies by village, ward, and land classification. Kerala applies a flat stamp duty rate of 8% — the same rate applies to male, female, and joint buyers, with no gender-based concession anywhere in the state.

Registration charges in Kerala are a flat 2% of the property value for all transactions, with no upper cap. Unlike many other states, Kerala does not levy a separate surcharge or cess on top of stamp duty for standard residential purchases. The total cost of buying and registering a property in Kerala is therefore a clean 10% of the property value — 8% stamp duty plus 2% registration.

Property Registration Process in Kerala

The registration process in Kerala is managed by the Inspector General of Registration through the IGR Kerala portal. Here are the key steps:

  • Step 1: Prepare the sale deed with the assistance of a registered document writer. The draft is vetted by the Sub-Registrar office for compliance.
  • Step 2: Pay stamp duty through e-stamping or on stamp papers purchased from authorised vendors. Online payment through the IGR portal generates an e-stamp certificate.
  • Step 3: Book an appointment at the Sub-Registrar office (SRO) having jurisdiction over the property location. The portal shows available slots.
  • Step 4: Appear before the Sub-Registrar with all original documents and witnesses on the scheduled date. Biometric verification and photography are completed.
  • Step 5: The Sub-Registrar verifies the deed, fair value compliance, and payments. Upon satisfaction, the deed is registered and a copy is provided.

Documents Required

  • Sale deed on e-stamp or stamp paper
  • Previous title documents (chain of ownership)
  • Encumbrance certificate
  • Land revenue records (adangal, basic tax register)
  • Property tax receipts
  • Aadhaar card and PAN card
  • Passport-size photographs
  • Identity proof of witnesses
