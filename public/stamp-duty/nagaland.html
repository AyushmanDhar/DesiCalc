Overview of Real Estate in Nagaland

Nagaland, with its capital Kohima and commercial hub Dimapur, has a distinctive real estate market shaped by its unique constitutional status under Article 371A. This provision protects Naga customary laws and practices, including land ownership and transfer. As a result, land in Nagaland is predominantly owned by individuals, clans, and villages under customary tenure, and transfers to non-Nagas are heavily restricted. Property prices in Dimapur are the highest in the state due to its commercial importance and connectivity, while Kohima offers a more moderate market driven by government employment and educational institutions.

Most residential land in Kohima and Dimapur falls under customary tenure, which means a No Objection Certificate from the village council is required before registration. Non-Naga buyers additionally need clearance from the Home Department. For those who can transact, Nagaland's stamp duty is a uniform 8.25% for all buyers — one of the highest flat rates in the Northeast — with no gender concession.

How Stamp Duty is Calculated in Nagaland

Stamp duty in Nagaland is calculated on the higher of the circle rate (guidance value) or the actual sale price. The rate is a flat 8.25% of the property value for all buyers — male, female, and every joint-ownership combination — with no gender-based differentiation and no value slabs. The full property value is taxed at the single applicable rate.

The registration fee is a flat 1% of the property value with no upper cap, bringing the total transaction cost to 9.25% for all buyer categories. No additional surcharges apply. Property Registration Process in Nagaland

Property registration in Nagaland is handled by the Sub-Registrar offices under the Department of Land Resources. For customary land (most of Kohima and Dimapur), the process requires:

  1. Step 1: Obtain NOC from the village council confirming the seller's ownership rights and no existing disputes.
  2. Step 2: Non-Naga buyers must additionally obtain clearance from the Home Department, Government of Nagaland.
  3. Step 3: Purchase e-stamp paper of the appropriate value through authorised banks.
  4. Step 4: Execute the sale deed and present it at the Sub-Registrar office with both parties and two witnesses.
  5. Step 5: Sub-Registrar verifies documents and completes registration. Process takes 15–30 days due to customary clearance requirements.

Documents Required

  • Sale deed on e-stamp paper of appropriate denomination
  • Previous title deed or grant certificate
  • Encumbrance certificate for the last 13 years
  • Land holding certificate from the village council
  • Tax receipts and dues clearance certificate
  • Identity proof (Aadhaar, Voter ID) of all parties
  • PAN card of buyer and seller
  • Passport-size photographs
  • NOC from the village council (for customary land)
  • NOC from the Home Department (for non-Naga buyers)
