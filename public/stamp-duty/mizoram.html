Overview of Real Estate in Mizoram

Mizoram, a picturesque state in Northeast India, has a developing real estate market centered around its capital Aizawl and emerging towns like Lunglei and Champhai. Property prices in Mizoram are relatively affordable compared to the national average, though Aizawl has seen steady appreciation due to urbanization and limited land availability. The state's unique landholding system is governed by customary laws and the Sixth Schedule of the Constitution, with the Autonomous District Councils playing a crucial role in land administration. The majority of land in Mizoram is owned by the state or by communities under traditional tenure systems.

Aizawl, perched on a ridge in the Lushai Hills, is the dominant property market, with demand driven by government employment, educational institutions, and the state's expanding service sector. The steady digitisation of land records through the Digital India Land Records Modernisation Programme (DILRMP) is gradually improving title transparency. For buyers, the most relevant recent change is the 2024 revision of stamp duty, which raised the conveyance rate from 1% to 3% — still among the lowest registration costs in India.

How Stamp Duty is Calculated in Mizoram

Stamp duty in Mizoram is charged on the higher of the circle rate (guidance value) or the actual transaction value of the property. Under the Indian Stamp (Mizoram Amendment) Act, 2024 (Act No. 4 of 2024, gazetted 15 March 2024), the stamp duty on a conveyance is a flat 3% of the market value for all buyers — there is no gender-based differentiation, and the full value is taxed at the single flat rate with no value slabs.

The only concession is a 2.5% rate for a widow who is the sole purchaser, as defined in the 2024 Act (a woman who lost her spouse through death, divorce or any other means and lives in a separate household, or a woman with children out of marriage living in a separate household). A flat registration fee of 1% applies on the property value, bringing the total cost to 4%. There are no additional surcharges or cesses applicable in Mizoram, making the total cost straightforward to calculate.

Property Registration Process in Mizoram

The registration of property in Mizoram is carried out under the Indian Registration Act, 1908, through the offices of the Sub-Registrar. Here are the key steps:

  • Step 1: Execute a sale deed on non-judicial stamp paper or e-stamp paper of the appropriate value with the assistance of a legal professional.
  • Step 2: Pay the stamp duty and registration charges. Online payment options are limited — in-person payment at the Sub-Registrar office with physical stamp paper or franking is the most common method.
  • Step 3: Both the buyer and seller must be present at the Sub-Registrar office with two witnesses for the registration.
  • Step 4: The Sub-Registrar verifies the identity of the parties, checks the title documents, and ensures that all stamp duty and registration fees have been paid.
  • Step 5: After successful verification, the deed is registered and a certified copy is provided to the buyer. The process typically takes 7 to 14 working days.

Documents Required

  • Sale deed agreement on proper stamp paper
  • Certified copy of the previous title deed
  • Encumbrance certificate for last 12 years
  • Land pass book or patta certificate
  • Tax paid receipts
  • Approved building plan (for constructed property)
  • Aadhaar card, Voter ID, and PAN card of parties
  • Passport-size photographs
  • NOC from the land department if applicable
