Overview of Real Estate in Gujarat

Gujarat's property market spans Ahmedabad's booming periphery, Surat's thriving residential belts, and Rajkot's expanding urban core, with Vadodara and Gandhinagar following close behind. The state carries one of the most buyer-friendly stamp duty regimes among India's major states: at 4.9%, the total duty is meaningfully lower than Maharashtra's 6–7% or Karnataka's effective 7.5%, a factor that makes the state attractive for cross-state investment comparisons.

A defining feature of the Gujarat market is the jantri — the government's official minimum valuation per locality, revised periodically. A significant upward revision was implemented in 2023, with many areas seeing jantri values doubled or tripled relative to long-standing rates. Any buyer should check the current jantri for their property before budgeting, since an outdated estimate can materially understate the actual duty.

How Stamp Duty is Calculated in Gujarat

Stamp duty in Gujarat is a flat 4.9% — made up of a 3.5% basic duty plus a 40% surcharge (1.4%) — applied to the higher of the agreement (transaction) value or the jantri value for the locality. The same 4.9% applies to every buyer category, with no gender concession on the duty itself. There is no slab structure: the rate does not change with property value, and the same rate applies across all cities and districts including Ahmedabad, Surat, Rajkot, Vadodara, and Gandhinagar.

Registration charges are 1% of the property value. Gujarat channels its concession for women through the registration fee rather than the duty: sole female buyers (property registered in a single woman's name) can have the 1% registration fee waived entirely, bringing their effective total to 4.9%. Joint registrations, including female-female joint ownership, do not qualify for the waiver. Some property portals cite a 3.9% female rate — official IGR guidance as of 2026 keeps the duty at 4.9% for all buyers.

Property Registration Process in Gujarat

Property registration in Gujarat is administered under the Gujarat Stamp Act, 1958 (which adopts and amends the Indian Stamp Act, 1899) and the Registration Act, 1908, through the Inspector General of Registration, whose online portal is GARVI (garvi.gujarat.gov.in). Here are the key steps:

  • Step 1: Check the current jantri value for your property on the GARVI portal (searchable by taluka, village, and survey number) to confirm the duty base.
  • Step 2: Pay the stamp duty and generate the e-stamp (available through SHCIL or franking at designated banks and post offices) or pay online through the GRAS-Gujarat gateway.
  • Step 3: Use the GARVI portal to draft the deed, calculate the duty, and book a slot at the Sub-Registrar office.
  • Step 4: Buyer, seller, and two witnesses appear at the Sub-Registrar office with original documents and identity proof.
  • Step 5: The Sub-Registrar verifies the deed and payments; upon satisfaction, the deed is registered. Registration must be completed within four months of the instrument's execution.

Documents Required

  • Sale deed (executed on e-stamp or stamp paper)
  • Previous title documents (chain of ownership)
  • Encumbrance certificate
  • Jantri valuation certificate
  • Property tax receipts
  • Aadhaar card and PAN card of buyer and seller
  • Passport-size photographs of parties and witnesses
  • Identity and address proof of buyer, seller, and witnesses
  • Valuation certificate and power of attorney (if applicable)
