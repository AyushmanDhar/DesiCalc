Overview of Real Estate in Delhi

Delhi, the national capital, represents one of India's most dynamic and expensive real estate markets. The city is divided into multiple zones including South Delhi, West Delhi, North Delhi, East Delhi, Central Delhi, and New Delhi, each with distinct property characteristics. South Delhi, with areas like Defence Colony, Greater Kailash, Hauz Khas, and Saket, commands premium prices. West Delhi localities such as Janakpuri, Rajouri Garden, and Punjabi Bagh offer mid-range to premium options. North Delhi (Model Town, Kamla Nagar) and East Delhi (Preet Vihar, Patparganj) provide relatively more affordable options.

Delhi's real estate is governed by the Delhi Development Authority (DDA), the primary land-owning agency, which has developed numerous residential colonies and continues to launch housing schemes. The Delhi Master Plan guides urban development and zoning regulations. Property prices in Delhi are among the highest in India, with prime South Delhi localities commanding upwards of ₹20,000–40,000 per square foot. The Delhi Metro has significantly impacted property values, with areas near metro stations commanding substantial premiums. Newer growth corridors such as Dwarka, Narela, and the areas along the Dwarka Expressway offer comparatively affordable options with strong connectivity.

The circle rates in Delhi are determined by the Delhi government's Revenue Department and are revised periodically. These rates serve as the minimum valuation for property transactions, with Category A and B colonies (like Defence Colony, Greater Kailash) carrying the highest rates. The Delhi stamp duty structure offers a 2% concession for female buyers — among the most generous in India — making it significantly cheaper for women to register property in the capital.

How Stamp Duty is Calculated in Delhi

Stamp duty in Delhi is calculated on the higher of the agreement value or the circle rate. The standard rates in MCD areas are 6% for male buyers and 4% for female buyers. Joint ownership rates vary by composition: both female owners pay 4%, both male owners pay 6%, and mixed-gender joint ownership attracts 5%. Registration charges are 1% of the property value plus a flat ₹100 pasting fee, with no upper cap.

Delhi also offers a 1% first-time buyer rebate, which is modelled for female buyers: a first-time female buyer pays just 3% stamp duty. For example, on a ₹30 lakh property a first-time female buyer pays 3% stamp duty (₹90,000) plus 1% registration (₹30,000), for a total of ₹1,20,000. The total statutory cost therefore ranges from 4% (first-time female buyer) to 7% (male buyer) of the property value.

Two important points for 2026: the 20% rebate on circle rate valuations ended on 31 December 2025, so duty is now calculated on the full notified circle rate. And the rates above apply in standard MCD areas — NDMC areas (around 5.5% male / 3.5% female) and Delhi Cantonment Board areas (around 3% for all categories) follow separate, lower schedules.

Property Registration Process in Delhi

The registration process in Delhi is managed through the Delhi government's Revenue Department. Here are the steps:

  • Step 1: Prepare the sale deed and get it drafted by a registered deed writer or advocate.
  • Step 2: Pay stamp duty through e-stamping via SHCIL (shcilestamp.com) or the Delhi Revenue Department's DORIS portal.
  • Step 3: Book an appointment at the Sub-Registrar office through the DORIS online portal. Same-day appointments may be available based on slots.
  • Step 4: Appear at the Sub-Registrar office with all original documents. Both parties must be present with two witnesses. Biometric verification and photography are conducted.
  • Step 5: The deed is registered after verification. The registered document is digitally signed and can be downloaded from the portal.

Documents Required

  • Aadhaar card and PAN card
