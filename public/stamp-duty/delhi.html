Stamp duty rates in Delhi

Delhi has a relatively simple headline stamp duty structure, but the total cost includes a separate MCD transfer duty. The stamp duty rate depends on the gender and ownership pattern of the buyer, while the transfer duty rate depends on the same buyer category and on whether the property value is above ₹25 lakh.

By buyer gender

A sole male buyer pays 6% stamp duty. A sole female buyer pays 4%, a two-percentage-point concession. Joint female ownership also pays 4%, joint male ownership pays 6%, and a mixed male and female joint purchase pays 5%. The transfer duty follows the same pattern at a lower scale: male buyers pay 3% up to ₹25 lakh and 4% above, while female buyers pay 2% up to ₹25 lakh and 3% above.

For a ₹60,00,000 property, a male buyer pays 6% stamp duty of ₹3,60,000 and 4% transfer duty of ₹2,40,000. A female buyer pays 4% stamp duty of ₹2,40,000 and 3% transfer duty of ₹1,80,000. The gap is substantial, which is why Delhi is often described as offering one of the strongest female concessions in India. The ownership should still be genuine and consistent with the funding.

By property location

Delhi's circle rates are set by the Revenue Department and vary by colony category. Colonies are grouped from Category A to Category H, with Category A localities such as Defence Colony and Greater Kailash carrying the highest values and Category H carrying the lowest. A property in a prime South Delhi colony can therefore have a much higher taxable value than a similarly sized property in an outer category, even though the stamp duty percentage is the same.

The MCD transfer duty applies to sales in Municipal Corporation of Delhi areas. For properties under other local bodies, confirm the applicable local levy and registration office with the Sub-Registrar before calculating the final cash outlay. New Delhi and NDMC areas can have different administrative arrangements, so the safest approach is to ask the specific Sub-Registrar office handling the property.

The Delhi Revenue Department's current property registration page describes stamp duty and transfer duty together at 6% for a male buyer and 4% for a female buyer. The calculator instead applies the separate stamp duty rates and the separate MCD transfer-duty schedule stored in its rate data. That is why the calculator total can be higher than the combined headline figure. Buyers should confirm the transfer-duty treatment with the Sub-Registrar before payment.

Registration charges

Registration in Delhi is 1% of the property value. The calculator applies no cap. The Delhi Revenue Department also mentions a ₹100 pasting charge on a sale deed, which is a separate administrative charge and is not included in the calculator. On a ₹60,00,000 property the calculator shows ₹60,000 registration; the pasting charge would be additional if the office applies it.

The calculator does not add any other stamp duty cess. The MCD transfer duty is shown separately because it is a distinct levy with its own value slabs. Legal fees, deed-writing charges, notary fees and society transfer charges are separate from both government charges.

How stamp duty is calculated in Delhi

The formula used by the calculator is stamp duty = taxable value × buyer-category rate, plus transfer duty = taxable value × transfer-duty rate, plus registration = 1% of taxable value. The taxable value is the higher of the circle rate and the actual sale consideration.

Circle rate vs market rate

Delhi circle rates are the minimum values notified by the Revenue Department for each colony category and property type. The market rate is the negotiated sale price. Stamp duty and transfer duty are calculated on the higher of the two. If the agreed price is below the circle rate, the circle rate is used; if the market price is higher, the market price is used.

This matters in several ways. A buyer who negotiates a price below the circle rate does not reduce the government charge. A buyer who pays a premium above the circle rate pays duty on the full premium. For inherited or gifted property, the valuation basis can be different, and a professional valuation may be needed. Always confirm the current colony category and circle rate before drafting the deed.

Other costs to budget for

Stamp duty, transfer duty and registration are the main government charges. Legal fees for title verification and deed drafting, notary charges, e-stamp facilitation, the ₹100 pasting charge where applied, society transfer fees and property tax arrears are separate. For DDA or society flats, check the lease terms, the permission-to-sell requirement and any outstanding dues before closing.

Documents required for registration in Delhi

Delhi registration is handled by the Revenue Department through the Sub-Registrar offices. The checklist can vary with the property and the local body, but the following documents are commonly needed:

  • Sale deed prepared on an e-stamp certificate
  • Previous title documents and the chain of ownership
  • Encumbrance certificate
  • Property tax receipts and house tax clearance
  • Aadhaar card and PAN card of buyer and seller
  • Passport-size photographs
  • Identity proof for two witnesses
  • NOC from the society or DDA where applicable
  • No dues certificate from the concerned authorities

For inherited property, keep the will, succession documents or family settlement. For a leasehold property, keep the lease deed and the lessor's permission where required. For a power-of-attorney transaction, keep the registered power of attorney and the principal's identity documents. Ask the Sub-Registrar office for its current checklist before the appointment.

Registration process in Delhi

Step-by-step registration

  1. Verify title and circle rate. Check the chain of title, encumbrance and the colony category circle rate for the property.
  2. Prepare the sale deed. A registered deed writer or advocate drafts the deed in the required format.
  3. Pay stamp duty and transfer duty. Use the Delhi e-stamping facility or a designated bank branch and keep the receipt.
  4. Book the Sub-Registrar appointment. Use the online appointment facility for the office with jurisdiction over the property.
  5. Attend with originals. Buyer, seller and two witnesses appear for biometric verification and photographs.
  6. Complete registration. The Sub-Registrar verifies the documents and registers the deed. The registered copy is made available digitally.
  7. Complete mutation and society transfer. Follow up with the revenue office and, for a flat, with the society or DDA.

Timeline and practical tips

The Delhi Revenue Department indicates a normal response time of about 15 days for registration. The appointment and verification may be completed earlier if the deed, e-stamp and documents are ready. Delays usually come from a valuation mismatch, missing society or DDA documents, or an incomplete transfer-duty payment. Keep certified copies of the registered deed and payment receipts for mutation, loan and resale.

Ways to reduce stamp duty legally in Delhi

Female and joint ownership

The female concession is the main legal route. A sole female buyer pays 4% stamp duty instead of 6% and a lower transfer duty. Joint female ownership pays the same 4% stamp duty and the female transfer schedule. A mixed male and female joint purchase pays 5% stamp duty but the male transfer schedule, so the saving is smaller than a sole female purchase. The first-time female rebate reduces the stamp duty further to 3% in the calculator.

Correct valuation and notified schemes

Use the correct circle rate and declare the real consideration. Under-declaration does not reduce the lawful charge because the department applies the higher value. If a DDA housing scheme, family transfer or other notified scheme offers a rebate, confirm the exact eligibility and documentation with the Sub-Registrar or a qualified advisor. The calculator applies only the published buyer-category rates, the female first-time rebate and the MCD transfer-duty schedule.
