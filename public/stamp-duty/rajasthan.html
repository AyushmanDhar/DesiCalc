Overview of Real Estate in Rajasthan

Rajasthan, India's largest state by area, has a diverse real estate market spanning its capital Jaipur, the industrial hub Kota, and heritage cities like Udaipur and Jodhpur. Jaipur has seen remarkable real estate growth with the development of areas like Vaishali Nagar, Mansarovar, and the upcoming Jaipur Metro project. The state's tourism-driven economy creates demand for heritage properties, hotels, and vacation homes.

Rajasthan has also emerged as a manufacturing and logistics hub, with industrial corridors driving commercial real estate demand. The state's NRI population, particularly in the UK and USA, invests significantly in Rajasthan's property market.

How Stamp Duty is Calculated in Rajasthan

Stamp duty in Rajasthan is calculated on the higher of the DLC rate (District Level Committee rate — what other states call circle rate or guideline value) or the actual sale price. The DLC rate is set by the Collector of each district and revised periodically. You can check current DLC rates on the official Epanjiyan portal (epanjiyan.rajasthan.gov.in).

Male buyers pay 6% stamp duty; female buyers pay 5% — a 1% concession. Joint ownership with all female co-owners is also 5%. Mixed-gender joint ownership is 5.5%, and joint ownership with all male co-owners is 6%. The registration charge is a flat 1% across all categories, with no upper cap.

Quick example: On a ₹50 lakh property in Jaipur, a male buyer pays ₹3 lakh stamp duty (6%) + ₹50,000 registration (1%) = ₹3.5 lakh total (7%). A female buyer pays ₹2.5 lakh + ₹50,000 = ₹3 lakh total (6%), saving ₹50,000.

Property Registration Process in Rajasthan

Property registration in Rajasthan is managed through the Epanjiyan portal run by the Department of Stamps and Registration. Here are the key steps:

  • Step 1: Draft the sale deed and submit it online via the Epanjiyan portal, or through a licensed deed writer.
  • Step 2: Pay stamp duty online through e-stamping on the Epanjiyan portal. The e-stamp certificate serves as proof of payment.
  • Step 3: Book a Sub-Registrar appointment through the portal. The portal shows available dates and slots.
  • Step 4: Both parties attend with original documents and two witnesses for biometric verification and photography.
  • Step 5: The Sub-Registrar registers the deed; download the registered copy from the portal.

Documents Required

  • Sale deed executed on e-stamp paper
  • Previous title deed and ownership chain documents
  • Encumbrance certificate for the last 13 years
  • Jamabandi or Khatauni (record of rights)
  • Latest tax receipts and dues clearance
  • Approved site plan or building plan (for constructed property)
  • Aadhaar card, PAN card, and Voter ID of all parties
  • Passport-size photographs
  • NOC from the relevant urban local body
  • Affidavit regarding possession and title
