Overview of Real Estate in Goa

Goa's real estate market is driven by tourism, NRIs and holiday-home buyers rather than local employment demand, which makes it unique among Indian states. North Goa — Panaji, Mapusa, Candolim, Calangute, Anjuna, Vagator and Assagao — sees the highest demand, while South Goa — Margao, Vasco da Gama, Palolem, Benaulim and Cavelossim — offers larger plots and quieter beachfront locations at comparatively lower prices.

Land supply is limited by strict rules: non-residents and even non-Goa residents face restrictions on buying agricultural land, and coastal regulation zones limit construction near beaches. Villas and apartments in gated communities dominate the market, and property prices in prime North Goa locations have risen steadily with the growth of luxury resorts and the airport's expansion at Mopa (Manohar International Airport).

How Stamp Duty is Calculated in Goa

Stamp duty in Goa is slab-based: 3% up to ₹50 lakh, 4% from ₹50 lakh to ₹75 lakh, 4.5% from ₹75 lakh to ₹1 crore, 5% from ₹1 crore to ₹5 crore, and 6% above ₹5 crore. On top of this, registration charges are a flat 3% of the property value at every slab — there is no cap and no separate cess.

All charges are calculated on the higher of the agreement value or the market value (collector's market value) prescribed by the government for that area. The same rates apply to male, female and joint buyers. On a ₹50 lakh property the total is 6%, rising to 8% at ₹1 crore and 9% above ₹5 crore.

Property Registration Process in Goa

Goa has moved property registration online through the NGDRS Goa portal, while stamp paper printing is handled by the Department of Printing & Stationery. Here are the key steps:

  • Step 1: Verify the title, encumbrance and market value of the property with the Sub-Registrar of the relevant taluka.
  • Step 2: Draft the sale deed and pay stamp duty through e-stamping — stamp papers are printed at the Department of Printing & Stationery and purchased from authorised vendors.
  • Step 3: Register the parties and upload documents on the NGDRS Goa portal (ngdrsgoa.gov.in), then book an appointment slot at the Sub-Registrar office.
  • Step 4: Both parties appear at the Sub-Registrar office with original documents and witnesses for verification and execution of the deed.
  • Step 5: After registration, mutation is recorded with the village panchayat or municipal authorities to update land records.

Documents Required

  • Sale deed on e-stamp paper
  • Previous title deed and ownership chain documents
  • Encumbrance certificate
  • Tenancy / mutation records (Form I and Form XIV)
  • Latest land revenue and tax receipts
  • Approved layout or construction plan
  • Aadhaar card, PAN card, and Voter ID of all parties
  • Passport-size photographs
  • NOC from the relevant authority if required (e.g. town and country planning)
