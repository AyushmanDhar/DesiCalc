Karnataka Stamp Duty Calculator 2026

Karnataka charges 2% stamp duty up to ₹20,00,000, 3% from ₹20,00,001 to ₹45,00,000 and 5% above ₹45,00,000, plus a surcharge of 12% of the stamp duty in urban areas or 13% in rural areas. Registration is 2%. There is no gender concession. The calculator applies the slab rate to the full property value, so a ₹60,00,000 property attracts 5% on the whole value.

Stamp duty rates in Karnataka

Karnataka uses a value-slab schedule rather than a gender-based one. The same rates apply to male, female and joint buyers. The slab rate is combined with a surcharge on the stamp duty amount and a 2% registration charge, so the all-in cost is higher than the headline slab percentage.

By buyer gender

There is no gender concession in the Karnataka calculator data. A male buyer, a female buyer and a joint purchase all use the same 2%, 3% or 5% slab. This is different from states such as Punjab, Uttar Pradesh and Delhi, where female buyers pay a lower rate. The absence of a concession means the ownership pattern does not change the stamp duty percentage in Karnataka.

Buyers sometimes ask whether a female co-owner can reduce the charge. Under the current calculator data, it cannot. The only way the rate changes is through the property value band: a lower-value property falls into the 2% or 3% slab, while a property above ₹45,00,000 falls into the 5% slab. The surcharge and registration are then applied on top.

By property location

The stamp duty slab does not change between Bengaluru, Mysuru, Mangaluru, Hubballi-Dharwad, Belagavi and other cities. What changes is the guidance value for the locality and whether the property is treated as urban or rural for the surcharge. The calculator uses 12% of stamp duty for urban properties and 13% for rural properties. A Bengaluru apartment normally falls in the urban category, while farmland or a rural plot may fall in the rural category.

Within Bengaluru, guidance values vary by ward and locality. Prime areas such as Sadashivanagar, Malleswaram and Indiranagar have high guidance values, while peripheral areas have lower values. The guidance value is the floor for stamp duty, so a higher-value locality produces a larger rupee charge even at the same percentage.

Registration charges

Registration in Karnataka is 2% of the property value. The calculator applies no upper cap. On a ₹60,00,000 property the registration fee is ₹1,20,000. This is separate from the 12% or 13% surcharge on stamp duty. The surcharge is calculated on the stamp duty amount, not on the property value, so it is much smaller than the registration charge on a high-value property.

The surcharge is an important part of the total. At the 5% slab, an urban surcharge of 12% adds 0.60% of property value, while a rural surcharge of 13% adds 0.65%. The calculator includes this amount in the cess line and in the total. No other state-level cess is added by the calculator for Karnataka.

How stamp duty is calculated in Karnataka

The calculator first selects the slab rate from the property value, then applies that rate to the full value. It then calculates the surcharge as a percentage of the stamp duty amount and adds registration at 2% of the property value. The formula is: stamp duty = property value × slab rate; surcharge = stamp duty × 12% or 13%; registration = property value × 2%.

Circle rate vs market rate

Karnataka calls its circle rate the guidance value. The Department of Stamps and Registration publishes guidance values by locality, property type and land use. Stamp duty is calculated on the higher of the guidance value and the actual sale consideration. If the agreed price is below the guidance value, the guidance value is used. If the market price is higher, the market price is used.

The guidance value is not the same as the market price. Prime localities can trade well above the guidance value, while some peripheral or distressed properties may trade below it. The department uses the higher value to prevent under-declaration. Buyers should check the current guidance value on the Kaveri portal or at the Sub-Registrar office before finalising the deed.

Other costs to budget for

Stamp duty, surcharge and registration are the main government charges. A property purchase also involves legal fees for title verification and deed drafting, notary and e-stamp facilitation charges, khata transfer fees, society transfer charges and any property tax arrears. For an apartment, check the maintenance dues and the society NOC. For a plot, check the khata, conversion order and encumbrance status.

Documents required for registration in Karnataka

Karnataka registration is handled by the Department of Stamps and Registration through the Kaveri system. The checklist can vary with the property, but the following documents are commonly needed:

  • Sale deed printed on e-stamp paper generated through the Kaveri portal
  • Previous title documents and the chain of ownership
  • Encumbrance certificate
  • Property tax paid receipts and the latest tax paid certificate
  • Khata certificate and khata extract from the municipal authority
  • Aadhaar card and PAN card of buyer and seller
  • Passport-size photographs
  • Identity proof for two witnesses
  • NOC from the apartment association where applicable

For inherited property, keep the will, succession certificate or family settlement. For agricultural land, keep the conversion order and the revenue records. For an apartment, keep the sale agreement, the undivided share of land details and the occupation certificate where available. Ask the Sub-Registrar office for its current checklist before the appointment.

Registration process in Karnataka

Step-by-step registration

  1. Check the guidance value. Use the Kaveri portal or the Sub-Registrar office to confirm the value for the specific locality and property type.
  2. Draft the sale deed. A registered document writer or advocate prepares the deed and uploads the draft for approval.
  3. Pay stamp duty and registration. Use the Kaveri e-stamping and e-payment facilities and keep the receipts.
  4. Book the appointment. Choose a Sub-Registrar office with jurisdiction over the property and a convenient slot.
  5. Attend with originals. Buyer, seller and two witnesses appear for biometric capture, photographs and document verification.
  6. Complete registration. The Sub-Registrar verifies the deed and registers it. The registered document is made available through the Kaveri portal.
  7. Complete khata transfer and mutation. Follow up with the municipal authority and the revenue office to update the records.

Timeline and practical tips

The registration appointment is usually completed on the booked day if the deed, e-stamp and documents are ready. Delays commonly come from a guidance-value mismatch, missing khata documents or an incomplete e-stamp. Khata transfer and mutation are separate and can take additional weeks. Keep certified copies of the registered deed, payment receipts and the encumbrance certificate for future sale, loan and taxation needs.

Ways to reduce stamp duty legally in Karnataka

Value band and ownership structure

Karnataka has no gender-based rate, so the main legal lever is the property value band. A property priced at or below ₹45,00,000 stays in the 3% slab rather than the 5% slab, although the actual price and guidance value determine whether that is possible. Splitting a transaction artificially to fall into a lower band is not a legitimate planning technique and can attract scrutiny. The ownership structure should follow the genuine commercial and family arrangement.

Correct valuation and notified exemptions

Use the correct guidance value and declare the real consideration. Under-declaration does not reduce the lawful charge because the department applies the higher value. If a transfer falls within a family settlement, gift or notified exemption, confirm the exact eligibility and documentation with the Sub-Registrar or a qualified advisor. The calculator applies only the published slab, surcharge and registration rates.
