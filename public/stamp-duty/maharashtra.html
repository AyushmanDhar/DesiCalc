Stamp duty rates in Maharashtra

Maharashtra does not have one statewide stamp duty percentage. The rate depends on the local body that governs the property, and Mumbai adds a separate metro cess. This is why two properties with the same sale price can attract very different stamp duty amounts in Mumbai, Pune and a smaller municipal council area.

By buyer gender

Female buyers receive a one-percentage-point concession in every location category. In Mumbai the base rate is 5% for men and 4% for women, and the metro cess is added to both. In Pune and the other municipal corporations the rate is 7% for men and 6% for women. Municipal council areas use 4% and 3%, while gram panchayats use 3% and 2%. A mixed male and female joint purchase sits between the male and female rates: 4.5% in Mumbai, 6.5% in Pune, 3.5% in municipal council areas and 2.5% in gram panchayats.

The concession applies to the stamp duty rate, not to the registration fee. Registration remains 1% of value, capped at ₹30,000, regardless of buyer gender. For a family purchase, the ownership pattern should match the actual contribution and the intended succession. A female co-owner can reduce the duty, but the deed should not misstate the funding or possession arrangement.

By property location

Mumbai municipal corporation limits use the lowest base rates in the state, but the 1% metro cess brings the all-in rate to 6% for men, 5% for women and 5.5% for mixed joint ownership. Pune, Thane, Nagpur, Navi Mumbai and Pimpri-Chinchwad use the higher municipal corporation rate of 7%, 6% and 6.5% with no metro cess. Municipal council, panchayat and cantonment areas use 4%, 3% and 3.5%, and gram panchayat areas use 3%, 2% and 2.5%.

Location also changes the ready reckoner value on which duty is calculated. A Mumbai flat in a prime ward can have a ready reckoner value many times higher than a similarly sized flat in a distant suburb or in a smaller city. The percentage rate and the valuation base therefore work together. Buyers should check both the local body classification and the ready reckoner rate for the specific ward or village.

Registration charges

Registration in Maharashtra is 1% of the property value, capped at ₹30,000. The cap is reached at a property value of ₹30,00,000. Above that value the fee remains ₹30,000, so the effective registration rate falls as the property value rises. On a ₹60,00,000 property the fee is ₹30,000, not ₹60,000. On a ₹1,00,00,000 property it is still ₹30,000.

The metro cess is the only city-specific cess in the calculator data. It applies to Mumbai municipal corporation limits at 1% of property value and is already reflected in the Mumbai rates shown above. No local body cess is added for Pune, Thane, Nagpur, Navi Mumbai, Pimpri-Chinchwad, municipal council areas or gram panchayats. Legal fees, deed-writing charges and society transfer fees are separate.

How stamp duty is calculated in Maharashtra

The formula is stamp duty = higher of ready reckoner value and sale consideration × location rate. Mumbai adds 1% metro cess on the same value. Registration is 1% of the value, capped at ₹30,000. The total government charge is stamp duty plus metro cess plus registration.

Circle rate vs market rate

Maharashtra calls its circle rate the ready reckoner rate or ASR rate. The Inspector General of Registration and Controller of Stamps publishes these values by city, ward, locality and property type. The rate is revised periodically, usually with effect from April. Stamp duty is paid on the higher of the ready reckoner value and the actual sale consideration.

If the agreed price is below the ready reckoner rate, the department will use the ready reckoner value. If the market price is higher, the market price is used. This is particularly important in Mumbai and Pune, where ready reckoner values can change sharply between adjacent wards. The e-ASR system maintained by IGR Maharashtra can be used to check the applicable value before the deed is drafted.

Other costs to budget for

The calculator covers stamp duty, metro cess and registration. A real transaction also involves legal fees for title search and deed drafting, notary and e-stamp facilitation charges, society NOC and transfer fees, and any arrears of property tax or maintenance. These are not government stamp duty and should be budgeted separately. For a flat, the society transfer fee and the share-certificate transfer can be a meaningful additional cost.

Documents required for registration in Maharashtra

Maharashtra registration is handled through the Inspector General of Registration and Controller of Stamps. The Sub-Registrar's checklist can vary with the property, but the following documents are commonly needed:

  • Sale deed prepared on e-stamp paper of the correct value
  • Previous title documents and the chain of ownership
  • Encumbrance certificate or property search report
  • Property tax receipts and dues clearance
  • Non-agriculture order for agricultural land converted to residential use
  • Society NOC and share certificate for a flat purchase
  • Aadhaar card and PAN card of buyer and seller
  • Passport-size photographs and identity proof for two witnesses
  • Power of attorney, if either party is represented

For inherited or jointly held property, expect succession documents, the family settlement or the registered will. For a redevelopment or under-construction flat, expect the agreement for sale, the development agreement and the occupation certificate where available. Ask the Sub-Registrar office for its current checklist before the appointment.

Registration process in Maharashtra

Step-by-step registration

  1. Check the ready reckoner value. Use the IGR Maharashtra e-ASR facility to confirm the value for the specific ward, village and property type.
  2. Draft the sale deed. A registered document writer or advocate prepares the deed, including the property description, consideration and ownership shares.
  3. Pay stamp duty and registration. Use the e-stamping and e-payment facilities integrated with the IGR Maharashtra system. Keep the payment receipts.
  4. Book an e-appointment. Choose a Sub-Registrar office with jurisdiction over the property and a convenient slot.
  5. Attend with originals. Buyer, seller and two witnesses appear for biometric capture, photographs and document verification.
  6. Complete registration. The Sub-Registrar verifies the deed and registers it. The registered copy is made available through the portal.
  7. Complete mutation and society transfer. Follow up with the local revenue office and, for a flat, with the society for transfer of the share certificate.

Timeline and practical tips

The registration appointment is usually completed on the booked day if the deed, payment and documents are ready. Delays commonly come from a mismatch between the deed value and the ready reckoner value, missing society documents, or an incomplete e-stamp. Mutation and society transfer are separate and can take additional weeks. Keep certified copies of the registered deed, payment receipts and the encumbrance search in a safe place.

Ways to reduce stamp duty legally in Maharashtra

Female and joint ownership

The simplest legal route is the female concession. A woman pays one percentage point less than a man in the same location. A mixed male and female joint purchase pays a rate between the two. On a ₹60,00,000 Pune property, a male buyer pays ₹4,20,000 stamp duty while a female buyer pays ₹3,60,000, a difference of ₹60,000 before registration. In Mumbai the difference is also one percentage point of value, plus the same metro cess.

The ownership pattern should reflect the actual funding and the intended succession. Adding a female co-owner solely to obtain the lower rate, without a genuine transfer of ownership or contribution, can create disputes and may not be accepted if the transaction is examined. Take legal advice before restructuring ownership within a family.

Correct valuation and notified concessions

Use the correct ready reckoner value and declare the real consideration. Under-declaration does not reduce the lawful duty because the department applies the higher value. If a transaction falls under a notified affordable-housing, redevelopment or family-transfer concession, confirm the exact eligibility, carpet-area limit, income limit and date window with the Sub-Registrar or a qualified advisor. The calculator applies only the published location and gender rates; it does not model every scheme.
