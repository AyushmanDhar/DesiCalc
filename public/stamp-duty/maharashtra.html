Overview of Real Estate in Maharashtra

Maharashtra is India's largest real estate market by value, anchored by Mumbai — the country's financial capital and its most expensive property market. The Mumbai Metropolitan Region (MMR) spans Mumbai city, Thane, Navi Mumbai, and extends along the Western Express Highway towards Virar and Panvel. Pune has emerged as a major IT and manufacturing hub with rapidly appreciating property in Hinjewadi, Baner, Kharadi and the airport corridor, while Nagpur, Nashik and Aurangabad offer more affordable options in the state's interior.

Maharashtra's stamp duty structure combines gender-based rates with city-level cesses. Beyond the base stamp duty, municipal corporations levy a 1% metro cess, and Pune, Thane and Nagpur add a further 1% Local Body Tax (LBT). Buyers also pay 1% registration charges capped at ₹30,000. Property taxes and stamp duty are computed on the higher of the agreement value or the government's ready reckoner (circle) rate, which is revised annually.

How Stamp Duty is Calculated in Maharashtra

Maharashtra uses a gender-based stamp duty model: 5% for male buyers, 4% for female buyers and 4.5% for joint male + female buyers (Mumbai model). On top of the stamp duty, a 1% metro cess is added in municipal corporation areas, giving effective rates of 6%, 5% and 5.5% respectively. Registration charges are 1% of the property value, capped at ₹30,000.

In Pune, Thane and Nagpur, an additional 1% Local Body Tax (LBT) is charged, so a male buyer in Pune pays 7% (6% effective stamp duty + 1% LBT) plus registration. All charges are calculated on the higher of the agreement value or the ready reckoner value. A male buyer of a ₹50 lakh property in Mumbai pays ₹3,30,000 in total (6.6%), while a female buyer pays ₹2,80,000 (5.6%).

Property Registration Process in Maharashtra

Maharashtra's registration process is managed by the Inspector General of Registration (IGR) through the official portal. Here are the key steps:

  • Step 1: Verify the title, encumbrance and ready reckoner value of the property on the IGR Maharashtra website or at the Sub-Registrar office.
  • Step 2: Draft the sale deed and pay stamp duty through e-stamping or franking at authorised banks, or online through the IGR portal.
  • Step 3: Book a slot at the Sub-Registrar office online through the IGR Maharashtra portal (igrmaharashtra.gov.in).
  • Step 4: Both parties appear at the Sub-Registrar office with original documents and two witnesses for biometric verification and execution.
  • Step 5: After registration, the deed is returned for mutation with the local municipal corporation or village panchayat to update land records.

Documents Required

  • Sale deed on e-stamp paper
  • Previous title deed and ownership chain documents
  • Encumbrance certificate (15 to 30 years)
  • Property card or 7/12 extract (land records)
  • Latest property tax receipts
  • Approved building plan and occupancy certificate
  • Society NOC (for flats in co-operative housing societies)
  • Aadhaar card, PAN card, and Voter ID of all parties
  • Passport-size photographs
  • Form 60/61 where PAN is not available

FAQs

In Mumbai and the Mumbai Metropolitan Region, stamp duty is 5% for male buyers, 4% for female buyers, and 4.5% for joint male + female buyers. A 1% metro cess is added on top in municipal corporation areas, making the effective rates 6% (male), 5% (female) and 5.5% (joint). Pune, Thane and Nagpur additionally charge 1% Local Body Tax.
Women pay 4% stamp duty in Maharashtra against 5% for men (Mumbai model), plus the 1% metro cess, giving an effective rate of 5%. On a ₹50 lakh Mumbai property, a female buyer pays ₹2,00,000 stamp duty + ₹50,000 metro cess + ₹30,000 registration = ₹2,80,000.
Registration charges in Maharashtra are 1% of the property value, capped at ₹30,000. Even on a ₹2 crore property, registration is limited to ₹30,000. On a ₹50 lakh property, registration is the full ₹30,000 cap.
On a ₹50 lakh property in Mumbai, a male buyer pays stamp duty of ₹2,50,000 (5%) + metro cess of ₹50,000 (1%) + registration of ₹30,000 (1%, capped) = ₹3,30,000 (6.6%).
On a ₹50 lakh property in Mumbai, a female buyer pays stamp duty of ₹2,00,000 (4%) + metro cess of ₹50,000 (1%) + registration of ₹30,000 (1%, capped) = ₹2,80,000 (5.6%).
The metro cess is an additional 1% charge on the property value levied in municipal corporation areas of Maharashtra, on top of the stamp duty. It was introduced to fund metro rail projects. In Mumbai the effective stamp duty becomes 6% for men and 5% for women after adding the metro cess.
Local Body Tax (LBT) is an extra 1% charged on the property value in Pune, Thane and Nagpur, in addition to stamp duty and the metro cess. This means a male buyer in Pune pays 6% stamp duty + 1% metro cess + 1% LBT + registration, while the same buyer in Mumbai pays no LBT.
The ready reckoner rate is the minimum circle rate fixed by the Maharashtra Inspector General of Registration (IGR) for property transactions in each area. It is revised annually and varies by locality, property type and floor. Stamp duty is calculated on the higher of the agreement value or the ready reckoner value.
Stamp duty in Maharashtra can be paid online through the IGR Maharashtra portal (igrmaharashtra.gov.in) using e-stamping or franking, or at authorised banks. Document registration can be initiated online with slot booking at the Sub-Registrar office, where both parties appear for biometric verification and final execution.
Required documents include the sale deed on e-stamp paper, previous title deeds, a 15-year or 30-year encumbrance certificate, the property card or 7/12 extract, latest property tax receipts, approved building plan and occupancy certificate, society NOC if applicable, Aadhaar and PAN of all parties, passport-size photographs, and Form 60/61 where PAN is not available.
