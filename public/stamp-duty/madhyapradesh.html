Overview of Real Estate in Madhya Pradesh

Madhya Pradesh's real estate market is anchored by Bhopal, the capital, and Indore, the state's commercial hub, with Gwalior, Jabalpur, and Ujjain forming active secondary markets. Indore has consistently ranked among India's fastest-growing cities, driving demand for residential and commercial space. Property prices in Madhya Pradesh remain moderate compared to the national average, and the state's strong connectivity — expressways, rail, and expanding airport infrastructure — continues to support values in urban corridors.

The state's stamp duty regime applies uniformly across all districts. Buyers should note that duty is computed on the government-fixed circle rate (guideline value) for the locality when it exceeds the declared transaction price, so checking the circle rate before budgeting is essential.

How Stamp Duty is Calculated in Madhya Pradesh

Stamp duty in Madhya Pradesh is a flat 7.5% applied to the higher of the agreement (transaction) value or the government-fixed circle rate (guideline value) for the locality. The same 7.5% rate applies to every buyer category — male, female, and joint ownership — with no gender concession. There is no slab structure: the rate does not change with property value.

Registration charges are 3% of the property value with no upper cap. For standard sale deeds, Madhya Pradesh levies no additional surcharge or cess on top of these two components — the combined 10.5% was restructured from the earlier combined rate of 9.5% when the state revised its local body levies. Gift deeds attract different duty (1% capped at ₹1,000 for family members, 5% for non-family).

Property Registration Process in Madhya Pradesh

Property registration in Madhya Pradesh is administered under the Madhya Pradesh Stamp Act and the Indian Stamp Act, 1899, together with the Registration Act, 1908, through the Registration and Stamps Department (MP IGR). The state's SAMPADA 2.0 portal handles online registration. Here are the key steps:

  • Step 1: Pay the stamp duty and generate the e-stamp (available through SHCIL) or purchase the stamp paper from an authorised seller.
  • Step 2: Visit the SAMPADA 2.0 portal on the MP IGR website (mpigr.gov.in), create an account, and enter the property details (district, locality, survey number, transaction value) to compute the duty.
  • Step 3: Pay online using net banking, debit card, or credit card and download the e-stamp and payment receipt.
  • Step 4: Book an appointment at the Sub-Registrar office and complete e-KYC for deed execution.
  • Step 5: Buyer, seller, and two witnesses appear at the Sub-Registrar office with original documents; upon verification, the deed is registered and a certified copy is provided.

Documents Required

  • Sale deed (executed on e-stamp or stamp paper)
  • Previous title documents (chain of ownership)
  • Encumbrance certificate
  • Circle rate / guideline valuation documents
  • Property tax receipts
  • Aadhaar card and PAN card of buyer and seller
  • Passport-size photographs of parties and witnesses
  • Identity and address proof of buyer, seller, and witnesses
  • Valuation certificate and power of attorney (if applicable)
