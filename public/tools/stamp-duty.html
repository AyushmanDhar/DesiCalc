What stamp duty covers

Buying property in India involves more than the agreed price. The state charges stamp duty on the sale deed, the registration office charges a fee to record it, and some cities add a cess or transfer duty on top. The calculator adds all of these into one total so you can see the real cost of the purchase. The rates in this article come from the same 2026 rate file that drives the calculator, so the explanation and the result panel never disagree.

Stamp duty

Stamp duty is the main state tax on the property instrument. It is charged on the higher of the circle rate, which is the minimum valuation the state fixes for a locality, and the consideration actually paid. If the sale agreement is below the circle rate, the duty is still worked out on the circle rate. Rates are set by each state and can change with the annual budget, so the same ₹60,00,000 flat can attract 1% in Sikkim and 9.9% in Meghalaya. Within a state the rate can also depend on the buyer's gender, the property's urban or rural location, and the value band.

Registration fee

The registration fee is charged by the sub-registrar to record the deed and issue a certified copy. It is usually a percentage of the property value, most often 1%, but the rate file ranges from 0.5% in Mizoram to 8.5% in Assam. Several states cap the fee: Himachal Pradesh and Manipur cap it at ₹25,000, Maharashtra and Mizoram at ₹30,000, Uttarakhand at ₹50,000, and Punjab at ₹2,00,000. Haryana instead uses a fixed slab from ₹100 to ₹50,000 based on value. Because the fee is separate from stamp duty, a low stamp duty state can still be expensive once registration is added.

Metro cess

A metro cess is an extra levy on property transactions in large urban areas. In the 2026 rate file it appears in Maharashtra, where Mumbai property attracts a 1% metro cess on the property value in addition to stamp duty and registration. The cess is charged on the full value, not on the stamp duty, so on a ₹1,00,00,000 Mumbai flat it adds ₹1,00,000 by itself. Other states fund urban infrastructure through transfer duty or a surcharge rather than a separately labelled metro cess.

Other charges

Transfer duty is a state levy on the transfer of ownership. Andhra Pradesh charges 1.5% of value, Telangana charges 1.5% in urban areas and nil in rural areas, and Delhi uses a slab of 3% or 4% depending on value and gender. A surcharge can apply on top of stamp duty: Rajasthan adds 33% of the stamp duty amount. Some states reduce the registration fee for women instead of reducing stamp duty, which is how Gujarat, Sikkim and Tamil Nadu are modelled. Tripura adds a small fixed charge of ₹78. The calculator shows these as separate lines so nothing is hidden inside the headline rate.

Worked example: ₹60,00,000 property in Punjab

Take a male buyer purchasing a ₹60,00,000 property in Punjab, with no first-time rebate and the default urban location. Punjab applies a flat 7% stamp duty for male buyers and 1% registration, capped at ₹2,00,000. The calculator returns the following figures:

Stamp duty₹60,00,000 × 7%₹4,20,000
Registration fee₹60,00,000 × 1%, below the ₹2,00,000 cap₹60,000
Cess, transfer duty and other chargesNone in Punjab₹0

The total of ₹4,80,000 works out to an effective rate of 8% on the property value, made up of 7% stamp duty and 1% registration. If the same property were bought by a woman, the stamp duty rate would fall to 5%, or ₹3,00,000, while registration would stay at ₹60,000, for a total of ₹3,60,000 and an effective rate of 6%. A male and female buying jointly are charged 6% in Punjab, which is lower than the male rate but higher than the female rate, so joint ownership does not always capture the full concession.

How to reduce stamp duty legally

There is no lawful way to pay less than the rate the state has notified for your transaction, but the rate itself depends on facts you can plan around. Gender, ownership structure and the recorded value all change the calculation. What does not work is understating the price: the duty is based on the higher of the circle rate and the consideration, and a mismatch can trigger a penalty or a notice from the registration department.

Women buyers

Many states charge women a lower stamp duty, and the saving is often one or two percentage points. In the 2026 rate file, Delhi, Haryana and Punjab offer a 2-point reduction, while Assam, Chhattisgarh, Maharashtra, Odisha and Rajasthan offer a 1-point reduction. Uttar Pradesh charges women 6% up to ₹1,00,00,000 instead of 7%, Uttarakhand charges 3.75% up to ₹25,00,000 instead of 5%, and Himachal Pradesh charges 4% up to ₹80,00,000 instead of 6%. Gujarat, Sikkim and Tamil Nadu keep the same stamp duty rate but reduce the registration fee for women. The concession must be claimed on the deed, so tell the sub-registrar's office before the document is prepared.

Joint ownership

Where a state offers a female-buyer rate, a deed in the name of two women can qualify for that rate. The rate file models this as joint female ownership, and in Punjab two female buyers pay 5%, the same as a single woman. A male and female joint purchase is treated separately: Punjab charges 6%, Delhi 5%, Uttar Pradesh 6.5% and Uttarakhand 4.37% up to ₹25,00,000. Adding a woman to the deed therefore lowers the rate in some states but not to the single-female level, and it changes the ownership share unless the deed records the shares explicitly. Get the ownership split and the rate confirmed in writing before registration.

Correct valuation

Stamp duty is charged on the higher of the circle rate and the consideration, so declaring a price below the circle rate does not reduce the duty. The sub-registrar will apply the circle rate and may also refer the case for undervaluation proceedings, which can add penalty and interest. The legitimate lever is the circle rate itself: check the notified rate for the exact locality, because adjacent colonies can fall in different valuation zones. If the agreed price is genuinely below the circle rate, the duty still follows the circle rate, so budget for that amount rather than the price on the agreement.

Frequently asked questions

What is stamp duty?

Stamp duty is a state tax paid on a written instrument that transfers property. For a sale deed it is a percentage of the property value and is collected at registration. The rate depends on the state, the buyer's gender and, in some states, the location of the property.

Who pays stamp duty, the buyer or the seller?

The buyer normally pays stamp duty and registration charges on a sale deed, although the parties can agree otherwise in the contract. On a gift, lease or mortgage the liability follows the instrument and the state stamp law. The calculator estimates the buyer's cost.

What is the difference between circle rate and market rate?

The circle rate is the minimum value the state fixes for a locality. Stamp duty is charged on the higher of the circle rate and the actual consideration, so a sale below the circle rate is still taxed at the circle rate. The market rate is what a willing buyer and seller agree, and it matters only when it is above the circle rate.

Is property registration mandatory?

Yes. Under the Registration Act, 1908, a sale deed for immovable property must be registered to transfer title. An unregistered sale deed does not convey ownership and is not admissible as evidence of the transfer. Registration also creates the public record used for future sales and loans.

Do women get a stamp duty discount?

Many states charge women a lower stamp duty. In the 2026 rate file the concession is 2 percentage points in Delhi, Haryana and Punjab, and 1 percentage point in Assam, Chhattisgarh, Maharashtra, Odisha and Rajasthan. Uttar Pradesh, Uttarakhand and Himachal Pradesh apply a lower rate below a value threshold. Gujarat, Sikkim and Tamil Nadu reduce the registration fee instead.

Is stamp duty tax-deductible?

Stamp duty and registration charges are not deductible from salary income. If the property is a self-occupied house, the charges can be added to the cost of acquisition when computing capital gains on sale, and they form part of the cost of the new house for the Section 54F exemption. For a business asset the charges are capitalised and depreciated under the Income-tax Act.
