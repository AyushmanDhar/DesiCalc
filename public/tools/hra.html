How HRA exemption works

House Rent Allowance (HRA) is part of your salary, but Section 10(13A) of the Income Tax Act lets you exclude the portion that pays for rented accommodation. The exemption is not the full rent and it is not the full HRA. It is the least of three annual amounts:

  1. Actual HRA received from your employer for the year.
  2. 50% of salary in a metro city, or 40% outside a metro city. For this rule, salary means basic salary plus dearness allowance that forms part of retirement benefits, plus any commission fixed as a percentage of turnover.
  3. Actual rent paid minus 10% of salary. If this figure is negative, it is treated as zero, because a negative limit cannot increase your exemption.

The smallest of the three is your tax-free HRA. The remaining HRA is added to taxable salary. The calculator uses basic salary plus DA as the salary base because that is what most employers report in Part B of Form 16. If HRA is not a separate component of your salary structure, there is nothing to exempt under Section 10(13A).

Dearness allowance counts only when it forms part of retirement benefits; a DA that is fully consolidated and never counts for provident fund or pension is usually excluded by employers. Your salary certificate should show basic, DA, HRA and other allowances separately, because the exemption is worked out on those components and not on gross salary. Enter the monthly basic and DA exactly as they appear in your salary slip so the result matches Form 16.

Metro city definition

For HRA only four cities count as metro: Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata and Chennai. The 50% limit applies only in these four. Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune, Ahmedabad, Gurugram, Noida and every other city fall in the 40% bucket, however high the rent. This comes from Rule 2A of the Income Tax Rules and is unchanged for FY 2026-27. Selecting "Metro" in the calculator for a Bengaluru address would overstate the exemption and is one of the most common filing mistakes.

Worked example: Bengaluru rent of ₹25,000 a month

Take a non-metro employee in Bengaluru with basic salary of ₹50,000 a month, no DA, HRA of ₹20,000 a month and rent of ₹25,000 a month. For the year, basic salary is ₹6,00,000, HRA received is ₹2,40,000 and rent paid is ₹3,00,000. The three limits are:

All three limits meet at ₹2,40,000, so the full HRA is exempt and taxable HRA is zero. The rent of ₹25,000 is high enough that the 10% salary floor does not bite, but the exemption still cannot exceed the HRA actually received. In Delhi the 50% limit would be ₹3,00,000 and the answer would remain ₹2,40,000, capped by the HRA component of salary.

Documents required

Employers ask for rent receipts showing the landlord's name, address, the period covered, the amount paid and the landlord's signature. When annual rent crosses ₹1,00,000, the landlord's PAN is also required, and the rental agreement and bank transfer records should be kept ready for assessment. If monthly rent exceeds ₹50,000, tax is deducted at source under Section 194-IB at 2% of the rent, so the landlord's PAN is needed for that reason as well. Claim only rent that you actually pay; a rent receipt created without a real tenancy is a misrepresentation.

HRA under the new regime

HRA exemption is available only under the old tax regime. The new regime under Section 115BAC does not allow HRA, Section 80C, Section 80D or home loan interest. It does keep the ₹75,000 standard deduction and the employer NPS contribution under Section 80CCD(2). If your HRA and other old-regime deductions are large, the old regime can still win; otherwise the lower new-regime slabs usually cost less. Compare both results in the income tax calculator before choosing.

Frequently asked questions

Which cities are metro for HRA purposes?

Only Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata and Chennai are metro under Rule 2A. The 50% limit applies only there. Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune and every other city use the 40% limit, regardless of living costs.

Can I claim HRA under the new tax regime?

No. Section 10(13A) HRA exemption is available only under the old regime. The new regime allows the ₹75,000 standard deduction and employer NPS under Section 80CCD(2), but not HRA, 80C, 80D or home loan interest.

What if my rent is less than 10% of basic salary?

The third limit, rent paid minus 10% of salary, becomes negative and is treated as zero. In that case no exemption comes from that limit, and the exemption is the lower of the HRA received and the 50% or 40% salary limit.

What is Section 80GG and who can claim it?

Section 80GG is for people who pay rent but do not receive HRA. The deduction is the least of ₹5,000 a month (₹60,000 a year), 25% of adjusted total income, and rent paid minus 10% of adjusted total income. Form 10BA is required.

Do I need rent receipts to claim HRA exemption?

Yes. Employers ask for rent receipts showing the landlord's name, address, rent period, amount and signature. When annual rent exceeds ₹1,00,000, the landlord's PAN is also required. Keep the rental agreement and bank payment proof.

Can self-employed people claim HRA exemption?

No. Section 10(13A) applies only to salaried taxpayers who receive HRA. Self-employed professionals and business owners can instead examine the Section 80GG deduction if they pay rent and meet its conditions.
