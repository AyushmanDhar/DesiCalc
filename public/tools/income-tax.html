How income tax is calculated in FY 2026-27

Income tax is charged on your net taxable income, not on the salary figure in your offer letter. The calculator follows five steps: add up income, remove the standard deduction and eligible deductions, apply the slab rates, reduce the tax with the Section 87A rebate where eligible, then add surcharge and the 4% health and education cess. Every rate below comes from the calculator's own rate file, so the explanation and the result panel always agree.

Step 1: Gross total income

Gross total income adds salary, other income such as interest or rent, business income, and capital gains. The calculator keeps equity capital gains separate because they are taxed at special rates: 20% on short-term gains and 12.5% on long-term gains above the ₹1,25,000 exemption. The employer NPS contribution is shown on the form as a deduction under Section 80CCD(2), not as part of gross total income, because the employer's share is subtracted before the slabs are applied. If you have no salary income, the standard deduction is not available.

Step 2: Standard deduction

Salaried and pensioner taxpayers get a flat standard deduction of ₹75,000 under the new regime and ₹50,000 under the old regime. It is applied before any other deduction and does not require proof or investment. The old regime then allows deductions such as 80C up to ₹1,50,000, 80D health insurance, 80CCD(1B) NPS up to ₹50,000, home loan interest up to ₹2,00,000, and HRA exemption. The new regime allows only the standard deduction and the employer NPS contribution under Section 80CCD(2).

Step 3: Apply the slab rates

The new regime for FY 2026-27 uses seven slabs. Each rate applies only to the income inside that band, so a taxpayer in the 30% slab does not pay 30% on the entire income.

The old regime keeps the familiar three-slab structure for taxpayers under 60: nil up to ₹2,50,000, 5% from ₹2,50,001 to ₹5,00,000, 20% from ₹5,00,001 to ₹10,00,000, and 30% above ₹10,00,000. Senior citizens get a higher nil band of ₹3,00,000, and super seniors get ₹5,00,000. The calculator picks the correct old-regime slab set from the age you enter.

Step 4: Rebate under Section 87A

Section 87A reduces the tax itself, after the slabs have been applied. Under the new regime the maximum rebate is ₹60,000 and it is available when net taxable income is ₹12,00,000 or less. Under the old regime the maximum rebate is ₹12,500 and the income limit is ₹5,00,000. The rebate is the lower of the tax before rebate and the cap, so it can never create a refund. For income just above ₹12,00,000 in the new regime, marginal relief caps the tax at the amount by which income exceeds ₹12,00,000. The calculator shows that relief on its own line when it applies.

Step 5: Surcharge and cess

Surcharge is an extra charge on tax for higher incomes. Under the new regime it is nil up to ₹50,00,000, 10% from ₹50,00,001 to ₹1,00,00,000, 15% from ₹1,00,00,001 to ₹2,00,00,000, and 25% above ₹2,00,00,000. The old regime uses the same bands but adds a 37% rate above ₹5,00,00,000 and keeps 25% for income from ₹2,00,00,001 to ₹5,00,00,000. Marginal relief also applies near each surcharge threshold so that crossing a threshold by a small amount does not produce a disproportionate jump.

Old regime vs new regime

The new regime is the default because its slabs and ₹75,000 standard deduction usually win for taxpayers who do not claim large deductions. The old regime can still be better when you have substantial 80C investments, 80D health premiums, home loan interest under Section 24(b), or HRA exemption under Section 10(13A). Those deductions reduce net taxable income before the slabs are applied, so their value depends on your marginal rate. Compare both results in the calculator and read the detailed old vs new tax regime comparison before choosing.

Common mistakes

Mixing regime deduction rules

A common error is to enter 80C, 80D, home loan interest or HRA under the new regime and expect them to reduce tax. The new regime allows only the standard deduction and the employer NPS contribution under Section 80CCD(2). The old-regime deduction fields are hidden when the new regime is selected for exactly this reason. Switching regimes without switching the deduction assumptions can make the comparison look better than it really is.

Ignoring marginal relief

Income just above ₹12,00,000 does not lose the entire benefit at once. Marginal relief limits the tax to the amount by which net taxable income exceeds ₹12,00,000, so the jump at the threshold is gradual. A similar relief applies near the surcharge thresholds. Do not assume that earning one rupee more than a threshold automatically creates a large tax bill; check the marginal relief line in the result panel.

Forgetting capital gains rates

Equity short-term capital gains are taxed at 20% and equity long-term capital gains at 12.5% above the ₹1,25,000 exemption. These rates sit outside the slab table, so adding capital gains to salary income and applying only slab rates will understate the tax. The calculator handles them separately and adds the special-rate tax to the slab tax before applying the rebate.

Frequently asked questions

What income is tax-free in new regime?

Under the new regime, a resident individual pays no tax on net taxable income up to ₹12,00,000 because the Section 87A rebate of up to ₹60,000 offsets the slab tax. A salaried employee also gets the ₹75,000 standard deduction, so gross salary up to ₹12,75,000 can be tax-free.

Is old regime better with home loan?

The old regime allows deduction of up to ₹2,00,000 of home loan interest on a self-occupied property under Section 24(b). If your interest, 80C investments and 80D premiums together exceed the extra tax benefit of the new regime, the old regime can cost less.

How is 87A rebate calculated?

The rebate is the lower of your tax before rebate or the maximum rebate. Under the new regime the cap is ₹60,000 and it applies when net taxable income is ₹12,00,000 or less. Under the old regime the cap is ₹12,500 and it applies when net taxable income is ₹5,00,000 or less.

What is the standard deduction for FY 2026-27?

Salaried and pensioner taxpayers get ₹75,000 under the new regime and ₹50,000 under the old regime. The deduction is available only when salary income is present, and it is applied before the slab rates.

Who is eligible for the 87A rebate?

The rebate is available to resident individuals. Under the new regime it applies when net taxable income is up to ₹12,00,000. Under the old regime it applies when net taxable income is up to ₹5,00,000. Non-residents and entities such as companies and firms cannot claim it.

What are the surcharge thresholds?

Under the new regime surcharge is nil up to ₹50,00,000, 10% above ₹50,00,000 up to ₹1,00,00,000, 15% above ₹1,00,00,000 up to ₹2,00,00,000, and 25% above ₹2,00,00,000. The old regime adds a 37% rate above ₹5,00,00,000; the new regime caps surcharge at 25%.

Can I claim HRA under the new regime?

No. HRA exemption under Section 10(13A) is available only in the old regime. The new regime does not allow HRA, 80C, 80D or home loan interest deductions, but it does keep the ₹75,000 standard deduction and the employer NPS deduction under Section 80CCD(2).
