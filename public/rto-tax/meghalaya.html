Vehicle Registration Process in Meghalaya

Meghalaya, known as the "Abode of Clouds," has a well-structured vehicle registration system administered by the Meghalaya Transport Department. The state is divided into several RTO jurisdictions including Shillong (ML 05), Tura (ML 07), Jowai (ML 04), Nongstoin (ML 10), Williamnagar (ML 11), Baghmara (ML 09), and Mawkyrwat (ML 12). The Shillong RTO in the East Khasi Hills district processes the bulk of registrations, catering to the state's largest urban population. Vehicle owners must submit their registration application within seven days of vehicle purchase. The process involves document verification, a physical inspection of the vehicle at the RTO office, payment of road tax and registration fees, and finally the issuance of a permanent registration number.

Tourism plays a significant role in Meghalaya's economy, and this is reflected in the high number of tourist taxi and rental vehicle registrations, particularly in Shillong, Cherrapunji, and the living root bridge areas. The state's terrain — a mix of the Khasi, Jaintia, and Garo Hills — makes SUVs and hill-capable vehicles popular choices. The Transport Department has been pushing for digitalisation, and many services including tax payments and form submissions can now be processed through the Meghalaya online transport portal, reducing the need for physical visits to the RTO.

Required Documents for Registration

  • Form 20 (duly filled application form)
  • Form 21 (sale certificate from the dealer)
  • Form 22 (manufacturer's roadworthiness certificate)
  • Valid insurance policy (third-party or comprehensive)
  • Address proof (Aadhaar, Voter ID, Passport, or utility bill)
  • Identity proof (Aadhaar, PAN, Driving Licence)
  • Original invoice and delivery challan
  • PUC certificate
  • Passport-size photographs (2 copies)

How Road Tax Is Calculated in Meghalaya

Meghalaya follows a slab-based road tax structure for cars — the same four slabs apply to petrol, diesel and CNG vehicles. Vehicles up to ₹3 lakh pay 4%, vehicles between ₹3–15 lakh pay 6%, vehicles between ₹15–20 lakh pay 8%, and vehicles above ₹20 lakh pay 10% of the ex-showroom price.

For example, a petrol SUV priced at ₹10,00,000 in Meghalaya falls in the ₹3–15 lakh slab and attracts a road tax of ₹10,00,000 × 6% = ₹60,000. Adding the registration fee of ₹600 and HSRP charges of ₹400, the total RTO cost comes to ₹61,000. A ₹25 lakh car pays 10% — ₹2,50,000 in road tax.

EV Exemption in Meghalaya

Meghalaya has implemented a full road tax exemption for electric vehicles, and the EV policy was renewed in July 2026 with the benefit extended — electric four-wheelers pay zero road tax and the registration fee is also waived, leaving only the ₹400 HSRP charge. This exemption is designed to promote cleaner mobility in Meghalaya's ecologically fragile hill regions. The state has also been encouraging the installation of EV charging stations in key tourist destinations like Shillong, Cherrapunji, and Tura to support the growing EV fleet.

Two-Wheeler Tax Rates

Two-wheelers in Meghalaya pay a fixed one-time amount based on unladen weight: ₹1,100 up to 65 kg, ₹1,800 for 66–90 kg, ₹2,500 for 91–135 kg and ₹2,990 above 135 kg. The registration fee is ₹300 and HSRP ₹200.

Commercial Vehicle Tax in Meghalaya

Commercial vehicles in Meghalaya pay tax based on seating capacity — small vehicles pay a one-time amount, while larger vehicles pay per seat per year:

Seats Tax
1–6 seats₹4,000 (one-time)
7–12 seats₹1,400 per seat per year
13 seats and above₹2,200 per seat per year
