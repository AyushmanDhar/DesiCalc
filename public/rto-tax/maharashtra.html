Maharashtra road tax by vehicle type

Maharashtra charges a one-time motor vehicle tax on private vehicles at registration, with separate slabs for petrol, diesel and CNG. A 2% Road Safety Cess is added to the tax for four-wheelers and two-wheelers, and the total motor vehicle tax is capped at ₹30 lakh.

Cars and SUVs

Petrol cars pay 11% up to ₹10 lakh, 12% from ₹10–20 lakh and 13% above ₹20 lakh. Diesel cars pay 13%, 14% and 15% in the same bands; CNG cars pay 8%, 9% and 10%. A ₹8 lakh petrol car pays ₹8,00,000 × 11% = ₹88,000 plus 2% cess of ₹1,760, or ₹89,760; with ₹600 registration and ₹400 HSRP the total is ₹90,760. The cap of ₹30 lakh only affects ultra-luxury vehicles above roughly ₹2.3 crore.

Two-wheelers

Two-wheelers are taxed by engine capacity: 10% up to 99cc, 11% for 100–299cc and 12% for 300cc and above, with a minimum tax of ₹1,500 and the 2% cess on top. A ₹1,50,000 bike with a 150cc engine pays ₹16,500 tax plus ₹330 cess, or ₹16,830; registration is ₹300 and HSRP is ₹200, giving ₹17,330 at registration.

Electric vehicles

Electric vehicles get a 100% road tax exemption at first registration in Maharashtra, currently effective through March 2030 under the state EV policy. The exemption covers electric cars, two-wheelers and three-wheelers, but the ₹600 registration fee and ₹400 HSRP still apply. A ₹15 lakh electric car therefore pays ₹1,000 at registration instead of the ₹1,83,600 payable on a petrol car of the same price.

Commercial vehicles

Commercial vehicles up to 6 seats pay a one-time tax: ₹2,500 for 1–2 seats, ₹3,850 for 3–4 seats and ₹7,150 for 5–6 seats. Larger vehicles pay per seat per year — ₹1,700 for 7–12 seats, ₹3,000 for 13–24 seats and ₹5,000 for 25 seats or more. A 10-seat commercial vehicle therefore pays ₹17,000 a year, not a one-time amount.

Vehicle Registration Process in Maharashtra

Where to register

Maharashtra's vehicle registration is managed by the Maharashtra Transport Department, which operates one of India's largest RTO networks. Major RTOs include Mumbai (MH-01 to MH-04, MH-46, MH-47, MH-48), Pune (MH-12, MH-14), Thane (MH-05, MH-06), Navi Mumbai (MH-43), Nashik (MH-15), Nagpur (MH-31, MH-40), Aurangabad (MH-20), and Kolhapur (MH-08). Mumbai alone has multiple RTO zones due to the city's massive vehicle population.

The registration process follows the VAHAN portal framework. New vehicle buyers receive temporary registration from the dealer valid for 30 days. For permanent registration, you must submit Form 20 and supporting documents at the RTO. Given the high vehicle density in Mumbai and Pune, RTO appointments can have waiting periods. The RTO conducts physical verification before issuing a registration number in MH-XX-XXXX format. Maharashtra has implemented smart card RCs and online services extensively.

Required Documents for Registration

  • Form 20 (duly filled application form)
  • Form 21 (Sale Certificate from Authorised Dealer)
  • Form 22 (Manufacturer's Roadworthiness Certificate)
  • Valid Insurance Policy (minimum 1 year third-party)
  • Address Proof (Aadhaar, Voter ID, Rent Agreement, Utility Bill)
  • Identity Proof (Aadhaar, PAN, Passport)
  • Original Vehicle Invoice and Delivery Challan
  • PUC Certificate
  • HSRP receipt
  • Passport-size photographs (2 copies)

Fees and timelines

The registration fee is ₹600 for a non-transport light motor vehicle and ₹300 for a motorcycle. HSRP costs ₹400 for a four-wheeler and ₹200 for a two-wheeler. New vehicles get a dealer-issued temporary registration valid for 30 days, within which permanent registration must be completed. Mumbai and Pune RTOs can have appointment queues, so a direct application may take one to three weeks; dealer-assisted registration is usually faster.

How Road Tax Is Calculated in Maharashtra

Maharashtra uses a slab-based road tax system with different rates for petrol, diesel, and CNG vehicles. Petrol vehicles are taxed at 11% (0–₹10L), 12% (₹10–20L), and 13% (above ₹20L). Diesel vehicles attract higher rates of 13%, 14%, and 15% respectively. CNG vehicles enjoy lower rates of 8%, 9%, and 10%, making them the most affordable fuel option for registration. On top of the base rate, every four-wheeler pays a 2% Road Safety Cess on the road tax amount, and the total motor vehicle tax is capped at ₹30 lakh.

For example, an ₹8 lakh petrol car in Maharashtra costs ₹88,000 in road tax (11%) plus ₹1,760 cess (2%), totalling ₹89,760. A ₹15 lakh diesel SUV costs ₹2,10,000 (14%) plus ₹4,200 cess, totalling ₹2,14,200. A ₹50 lakh petrol car costs ₹6,50,000 (13%) plus ₹13,000 cess, totalling ₹6,63,000 — well below the ₹30 lakh cap.

EV Exemption in Maharashtra

Maharashtra offers a full 100% road tax exemption for electric vehicles at first registration, currently effective through March 2030 under the state's EV policy. For a ₹15 lakh EV, the saving compared to a petrol car of the same price is over ₹1.8 lakh. The exemption applies to all EV categories including cars, two-wheelers, and three-wheelers, and the ₹600 registration fee plus ₹400 HSRP still apply. Maharashtra is one of India's largest EV markets, with rapid adoption of EV taxis and electric two-wheelers in Mumbai and Pune.

Two-Wheeler Tax Rates

Two-wheelers in Maharashtra are taxed by engine capacity: 10% up to 99cc, 11% for 100–299cc, and 12% for 300cc and above, with a minimum tax of ₹1,500, plus the 2% Road Safety Cess on the tax. The registration fee is ₹300 and HSRP ₹200. Electric two-wheelers are fully exempt from road tax.

Frequently asked questions

Is road tax in Maharashtra one-time or annual?

Private cars and two-wheelers pay a one-time lifetime motor vehicle tax at registration. Commercial vehicles with up to 6 seats also pay one-time amounts, while larger commercial vehicles pay per seat every year.

How much is road tax for a ₹10 lakh car in Maharashtra?

For a ₹10 lakh petrol car, road tax is 11% (₹1,10,000) plus a 2% Road Safety Cess of ₹2,200, totalling ₹1,12,200. With ₹600 registration and ₹400 HSRP, the RTO total is ₹1,13,200. A diesel car pays 13% plus cess, and a CNG car pays 8% plus cess.

What is the road tax for two-wheelers in Maharashtra?

Two-wheelers pay 10% up to 99cc, 11% for 100–299cc and 12% for 300cc and above, with a minimum tax of ₹1,500, plus the 2% cess. A ₹1,50,000 150cc bike pays ₹16,500 tax plus ₹330 cess; registration is ₹300 and HSRP ₹200.

Are electric vehicles exempt from road tax in Maharashtra?

Yes. Electric vehicles registered in Maharashtra get a full 100% exemption from motor vehicle tax at first registration, currently effective through March 2030 under the state EV policy. The ₹600 registration fee and ₹400 HSRP charge still apply.

How much does HSRP cost in Maharashtra?

HSRP costs ₹400 for a four-wheeler and ₹200 for a two-wheeler. The plate is mandatory and is fitted by the dealer or an authorised fitment centre.

Can I get a road tax refund if I sell or transfer my vehicle?

Lifetime tax is not refunded on a sale inside Maharashtra; the tax remains with the vehicle. If the vehicle is moved to another state and re-registered there, you can apply to the original Maharashtra RTO for a refund of the unexpired portion with the NOC, re-registration proof and tax receipt. The RTO decides the eligible amount.

How do I register an out-of-state vehicle in Maharashtra?

Obtain the NOC (Form 28) from the original RTO, then apply for re-registration at the Maharashtra RTO that covers your address. The vehicle is inspected, road tax is calculated on the depreciated value at Maharashtra rates, and an MH registration mark is assigned.

What is the penalty for late registration or late tax payment in Maharashtra?

A new vehicle must be registered within 30 days. After that the RTO levies a late registration fee, and unpaid motor vehicle tax attracts penalty and interest under the Maharashtra Motor Vehicles Taxation Act. The exact amount depends on the delay and is calculated by the RTO.

What is the tax cap in Maharashtra?

Maharashtra caps the total motor vehicle tax at ₹30 lakh. Even if the calculated tax plus cess exceeds ₹30 lakh, only ₹30 lakh is payable. This cap mainly affects buyers of ultra-luxury vehicles priced above roughly ₹2.3 crore.

Can I register a vehicle in Maharashtra with a different state address?

No, you need valid Maharashtra address proof to register a vehicle in the state. A rental agreement or second-home address in Maharashtra can be used if it is genuine and supported by documents. Many buyers working in Mumbai and Pune register using their rental agreement as address proof.
