Understanding Road Tax in Punjab

Motor vehicle tax in Punjab is governed by the Punjab Motor Vehicles Taxation Act, 1924 (Punjab Act 4 of 1924) and the rules made under it. The Act gives the state government the power to fix tax rates by notification. Cars and motorcycles pay a one-time lump-sum tax on the vehicle's actual price (excluding taxes), while transport and commercial vehicles pay tax on a year-to-year basis.

Punjab revised its tax slabs for private vehicles in August 2024 — the first revision since February 2021. Under the notification issued by the Transport Department on 21 August 2024, new personalised four-wheelers pay 9.5% up to ₹15 lakh, 12% between ₹15 lakh and ₹25 lakh, and a new 13% slab above ₹25 lakh. Two-wheelers pay 7.5% up to ₹1 lakh, 10% between ₹1 lakh and ₹2 lakh, and 11% above ₹2 lakh (with a minimum of ₹22,000). The tax is calculated on the ex-showroom (actual) price of the vehicle before taxes.

A useful local rule: if you paid lump-sum tax on a car or motorcycle and later move out of Punjab with the vehicle — or sell it to a person residing outside the state — you can claim a refund of the lump-sum tax under Section 3(4) of the Act.

How Punjab Road Tax Is Calculated — Worked Examples

Road tax in Punjab is a simple percentage of the ex-showroom price: Road tax = ex-showroom price × slab rate. Registration fees and HSRP charges are added on top. Because there is no fuel-type distinction in the current slabs, a petrol and a diesel car of the same price attract the same tax.

  • Hatchback at ₹9,00,000 → 9.5% = ₹85,500 road tax. With a ₹600 registration fee and ₹566 HSRP set, the RTO cost is about ₹86,666.
  • SUV at ₹18,00,000 → 12% = ₹2,16,000 road tax.
  • Luxury car at ₹30,00,000 → 13% = ₹3,90,000 road tax.
  • Motorcycle at ₹1,20,000 → 10% = ₹12,000 road tax.
  • Premium motorcycle at ₹2,50,000 → 11% = ₹27,500 road tax.
  • Electric car at ₹15,00,000 → 0% road tax and no registration fee under the EV policy; only the HSRP set applies.

To put the 2024 revision in perspective: dealers in Ludhiana estimated that a four-wheeler costing ₹8 lakh now attracts around ₹4,000 more tax, a ₹14-lakh sedan is about ₹7,000 costlier, and a ₹30-lakh vehicle pays ₹60,000 more (Indian Express and The Tribune, August 2024). The state expects the revision to add about ₹70 crore a year to revenue.

Vehicle Registration Process in Punjab — Step by Step

Registration in Punjab runs through the VAHAN platform of the Parivahan portal, with most new vehicles registered at the dealer's point of sale. The official process, as published by the Punjab Transport Department:

  1. Buy from an authorised dealer. The dealer issues the sales certificate (Form 21) and the manufacturer's roadworthiness certificate (Form 22; Form 22A where the body is fabricated by a body builder).
  2. Apply in Form 20 within 7 days of delivery of the vehicle (excluding the period of journey) to the registering authority of your area.
  3. Dealer Point Registration. The dealer enters your owner and vehicle details on the VAHAN Dealer Point system, pays the registration fees and motor vehicle tax online (through SBI internet banking), and prints the filled Form 20 and Form 21.
  4. Inspection and approval. Dealers are notified as inspecting authorities for registration; the RTO approves the application, allocates the registration mark in serial order, and the smart-card registration certificate (Form 23A) is dispatched to you by courier or registered post.
  5. Temporary registration first. New vehicles are issued a temporary registration by the dealer, valid for a maximum of one month, at a fee of ₹50 under Rule 42(10) of the Punjab Motor Vehicles Rules, 1989 (unless exempted), plus the tax payable under the taxation Act.
  6. Fix the HSRP. High-security registration plates are mandatory for all vehicle classes in Punjab; for new vehicles the dealer fits them before delivery.

You cannot drive or keep a vehicle on a public road without it being registered under Chapter IV of the Motor Vehicles Act, 1988.

EV Road Tax Exemption in Punjab

Under the Punjab Electric Vehicle Policy, electric vehicles registered in the state are exempt from road tax and from the fees for new vehicle registration. This was notified by the Transport Department on 10 April 2023 (Notification No. 10/43/2018-2T2), and the exemption applies to EVs registered during the policy period. The policy, which focuses on cities like Ludhiana, Jalandhar, Amritsar, Patiala and Bathinda, aims to increase annual EV registrations by 25%.

In practice, an EV buyer in Punjab pays no road tax and no registration fee — the only RTO-linked charges are the HSRP set. EVs are also exempt from the new green tax on old vehicles (the green tax notification explicitly excludes battery-operated vehicles). Since the exemption is time-bound, check the current status on the official portal before you buy.

Green Tax on Old Vehicles in Punjab

Punjab levies a green tax on old vehicles, approved by the state cabinet in August 2024 and notified in January 2025, effective from 1 September 2024. Private vehicles older than 15 years pay it on renewal of the registration certificate; commercial vehicles pay it annually after 8 years:

  • Two-wheelers: ₹500 a year (petrol) / ₹1,000 a year (diesel)
  • Four-wheelers up to 1500 cc: ₹3,000 a year (petrol) / ₹4,000 a year (diesel)
  • Four-wheelers above 1500 cc: ₹4,000 a year (petrol) / ₹6,000 a year (diesel)
  • Commercial: motorcycle ₹250, three-wheeler ₹300, motor/maxi cab ₹500, light motor vehicle ₹1,500, medium vehicle ₹2,000, heavy vehicle ₹2,500 a year

Vehicles running on CNG, LPG, battery (EV) or solar power are exempt from the green tax. The government expects the green tax to raise about ₹34.4 crore a year.

Punjab Commercial Vehicle Road Tax

Commercial and transport vehicles in Punjab are taxed on a year-to-year basis under the Punjab Motor Vehicles Taxation Act, 1924, with rates fixed by class of vehicle — seating capacity, unladen/gross weight and permit type — rather than a flat percentage of price. Key structures:

  • Stage carriage buses are taxed per kilometre per day as determined by the state, with a one-time tax of ₹500 per km (₹250 per km for mini buses) on issue of a new stage carriage permit.
  • Tourist permit vehicles are taxed per seat per annum. In August 2024 the state cut these rates: ordinary bus ₹2,050, deluxe non-AC bus ₹2,650, deluxe AC bus ₹4,150 and super-integral coach ₹5,000 per seat per year (previously ₹7,000 per seat).
  • A special road tax is leviable on transport vehicles in addition to the ordinary tax, at rates not exceeding those in Schedule A of the Act.
  • Other-state transport vehicles become liable to tax the moment they enter Punjab.

Note: some older web pages quote a "10% Social Security Surcharge" on commercial vehicles in Punjab — we could not verify this from any official notification and have not repeated it here.

Tractors and Agricultural Vehicles in Punjab

Punjab's agricultural economy shapes its vehicle mix — tractors and farm machinery form a significant share of registrations in the Malwa belt. Under the taxation Act, a "four-wheeled personalised motor vehicle" explicitly excludes a tractor, and agricultural tractors used for farming are exempt from road tax. But the rules change when a tractor works commercially:

  • A tractor with a trolley used for commercial purposes attracts tax of up to ₹20,000 per annum or 20% of the vehicle's value, as per the Schedule to the Act.
  • For hauling food grains from mandis, the state has allowed tractor-trolleys to operate under a permit limited to a 25 km delivery radius (max 12 km on state/national highways), with a Board of Inspection certifying fitness before registration as a goods vehicle.
  • The Transport Department has also issued specific notifications for combine harvesters and for CEV (construction equipment vehicle) registration — check the official portal for the latest.

HSRP for tractors costs ₹191 (complete set), the same as two-wheelers.

Paying Punjab Road Tax Online

Road tax and registration fees in Punjab can be paid online through the VAHAN portal (vahan.parivahan.gov.in) — the official "Pay your Taxes & Fees" link on punjabtransport.org opens the same system. The steps:

  1. Visit vahan.parivahan.gov.in and select Punjab as the state.
  2. Choose the payment service (e.g., "Pay Vehicle Tax" for your registration class) and log in or continue as a guest.
  3. Enter your vehicle registration number and chassis number.
  4. Pay using the online gateway and download the receipt — the e-receipt is accepted as proof of payment.

At the time of a new purchase, the tax is typically collected by the dealer at the dealer point and remitted online, so it is already included in your on-road price. Owners of vehicles from other states can pay entry tax through the state's SLA portal / online instant tax payment facility on punjabtransport.org.

Transfer of Ownership in Punjab

When you buy or sell a used vehicle in Punjab, the transfer is reported under Section 50 of the Motor Vehicles Act, 1988: the seller reports the sale in Form 29 within 14 days, and the buyer applies for the transfer in Form 30 within 30 days (45 days for vehicles registered in another state). The Punjab Transport Department requires, along with the forms:

  • Certificate of registration and certificate of insurance
  • Pollution under control certificate and proof of residence
  • Affidavit of sale/purchase from both seller and buyer
  • No objection certificate from the financier (if the vehicle is financed) and from the original registering authority (for other-state vehicles)

Fees are charged as per Rule 81 of the Central Motor Vehicles Rules, 1989. Late reporting attracts a penalty as prescribed under Section 50(3) of the Act.

Fancy (Reserved) Registration Numbers in Punjab

Punjab allots registration marks of choice through an online e-auction of reserved numbers run by the Transport Department. Under the official process:

  1. Check availability of the last 4 digits you want on punjabtransport.org and register as a bidder with your email.
  2. Pay a non-refundable participation fee of ₹1,000.
  3. Bid in the weekly cycle — bidder registration runs Sunday to Tuesday, bidding Wednesday to Thursday, and the successful bidder pays the winning amount online Friday to Saturday.
  4. Download the allotment letter from the portal after approval.

Required Documents for Vehicle Registration

Keep this checklist ready for a new vehicle registration in Punjab (per the Transport Department's published requirements):

  • Form 20 (application for registration) — filled online at the dealer point
  • Form 21 (sales certificate from the dealer) and Form 22 / 22A (roadworthiness certificate)
  • Valid insurance certificate and pollution under control certificate
  • Proof of address (ration card, electricity bill, etc.) and identity documents
  • PAN card or Form 60 / 61 (as applicable)
  • Form 34 signed by owner and financier, plus financier NOC, if the vehicle is financed
  • Customs clearance certificate (imported vehicles) or original Form 21 from the authorities (ex-army vehicles)
  • Design approval from the State Transport Authority for trailers / semi-trailers

For temporary registration, Form 20 is submitted with the same certificates, and the fee is ₹50 as per Rule 42(10) of the Punjab Motor Vehicles Rules, 1989.
