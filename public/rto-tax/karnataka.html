Karnataka road tax by vehicle type

Karnataka's private-vehicle tax is a one-time life tax based on the ex-showroom price. An 11% Infrastructure and Road Safety Cess is charged on the life-tax amount, and a fixed Transport Workers Cess is added — ₹1,000 for four-wheelers and ₹500 for two-wheelers.

Cars and SUVs

Petrol, diesel and CNG cars pay 13% up to ₹5 lakh, 14% from ₹5 lakh to ₹10 lakh, 17% from ₹10 lakh to ₹20 lakh, and 18% above ₹20 lakh. The cess is charged on the tax, not on the car price. A ₹10 lakh car therefore pays ₹1,40,000 base tax, ₹15,400 cess and ₹1,000 Transport Workers Cess — ₹1,56,400 in total.

Two-wheelers

Motorcycles and scooters pay 10% up to ₹50,000, 12% from ₹50,000 to ₹1 lakh, and 18% above ₹1 lakh. The same 11% Infrastructure Cess applies, plus a fixed ₹500 Transport Workers Cess. A ₹1.5 lakh bike pays ₹27,000 base tax, ₹2,970 cess and ₹500 cess — ₹30,470 before the ₹300 registration fee and ₹200 HSRP.

Electric vehicles

Battery electric cars pay reduced slabs of 5% up to ₹10 lakh, 8% from ₹10 lakh to ₹25 lakh, and 10% above ₹25 lakh. The 11% Infrastructure Cess and ₹1,000 Transport Workers Cess still apply. Electric two-wheelers are exempt from the life tax, although the calculator applies the fixed ₹500 Transport Workers Cess.

Commercial vehicles

Commercial vehicles use a separate seating-based schedule: ₹2,500 one-time for up to 3 seats, then ₹1,500 per seat per quarter for 4–12, 13–20 and 21+ seat vehicles. Commercial tax is recurring and is not calculated from the private-car slabs.

Vehicle Registration Process in Karnataka — Step by Step

Where to register

Karnataka's vehicle registration is managed by the Karnataka Transport Department with RTO offices across the state. Major RTOs include the Bengaluru zonal offices, Mysuru, Mangaluru, Hubballi-Dharwad, Belagavi, Davanagere and Shivamogga. Bengaluru alone has multiple RTO jurisdictions because its vehicle population exceeds 1 crore.

The registration process follows the VAHAN portal framework. New vehicle buyers get temporary registration from the dealer valid for 30 days. Permanent registration requires submission of Form 20 and supporting documents. Given Bengaluru's high vehicle density, RTO appointments can have waiting periods. The RTO conduct physical verification of the vehicle before issuing a registration number in KA-XX-XXXX format. Karnataka has been at the forefront of RTO digitisation with most services available online.

Required Documents for Registration

  • Form 20 (duly filled application form)
  • Form 21 (Sale Certificate from Authorised Dealer)
  • Form 22 (Manufacturer's Roadworthiness Certificate)
  • Valid Insurance Policy (minimum 1 year third-party)
  • Address Proof (Aadhaar, Voter ID, Rent Agreement, Utility Bill)
  • Identity Proof (Aadhaar, PAN, Passport)
  • Original Vehicle Invoice and Delivery Challan
  • PUC Certificate
  • HSRP receipt
  • Passport-size photographs (2 copies)

Fees and timelines

The registration fee is ₹600 for a non-transport light motor vehicle and ₹300 for a motorcycle under Rule 81 of the Central Motor Vehicles Rules, 1989. HSRP costs about ₹400 for a four-wheeler and ₹200 for a two-wheeler. Dealer-point registration usually completes within a few working days; a direct RTO application may take longer depending on appointment and inspection. The temporary registration is valid for one month, so permanent registration should be completed within that window.

How Karnataka Road Tax is Calculated

Karnataka has one of the highest road tax structures in India. The state uses a slab-based system with rates starting at 13% for vehicles up to ₹5 lakh and going up to 18% for vehicles above ₹20 lakh. On top of these base rates, Karnataka levies an 11% Infrastructure and Road Safety Cess (computed on the tax amount) plus a fixed ₹1,000 Transport Workers Cess (₹500 for two-wheelers). This layered taxation makes Karnataka one of the most expensive states for vehicle registration.

Examples:

  • Car at ₹10 lakh → 14% = ₹1,40,000 + 11% cess (₹15,400) + ₹1,000 → ₹1,56,400
  • Car at ₹25 lakh → 18% = ₹4,50,000 + 11% cess (₹49,500) + ₹1,000 → ₹5,00,500
  • EV at ₹15 lakh → 8% = ₹1,20,000 + 11% cess (₹13,200) + ₹1,000 → ₹1,34,200

The official slab structure for non-transport vehicles in Karnataka (one-time tax):

  • Cars up to ₹5 lakh: 13%
  • Cars ₹5-10 lakh: 14%
  • Cars ₹10-20 lakh: 17%
  • Cars above ₹20 lakh: 18%
  • Two-wheelers up to ₹50,000: 10%
  • Two-wheelers ₹50,000-1 lakh: 12%
  • Two-wheelers above ₹1 lakh: 18%

All categories pay the 11% Infrastructure and Road Safety Cess on the tax and the fixed Transport Workers Cess on top.

EV Policy in Karnataka

Karnataka charges reduced slab rates for electric vehicles: 5% up to ₹10 lakh, 8% from ₹10-25 lakh, and 10% above ₹25 lakh — significantly lower than the 13-18% for ICE vehicles but not fully exempt. The 11% Infrastructure Cess and ₹1,000 Transport Workers Cess apply on top of the EV life tax. Electric two-wheelers are fully exempt from life tax. The Karnataka EV Policy aims to make the state a global EV manufacturing hub, leveraging Bengaluru's tech ecosystem, and offers incentives for EV manufacturing and charging infrastructure installation.

Two-Wheeler Taxation

Two-wheelers in Karnataka are taxed at 10% up to ₹50,000, 12% from ₹50,000 to ₹1 lakh, and 18% above ₹1 lakh, plus the 11% Infrastructure Cess on the tax and a fixed ₹500 Transport Workers Cess. A ₹1.5 lakh bike would attract 18% (₹27,000) + 11% cess (₹2,970) + ₹500 = approximately ₹30,470 in total tax and cesses. Electric two-wheelers are fully exempt from life tax.

Commercial Vehicle Tax in Karnataka

Commercial vehicles in Karnataka are taxed on a seating basis: a one-time flat amount of ₹2,500 for vehicles up to 3 seats, and quarterly per-seat tax of ₹1,500 per seat for 4-12 seats, 13-20 seats and 21+ seats alike.
